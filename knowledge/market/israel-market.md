# Israel: what changes for a Google Ads manager

This file covers what is different about running Google Ads for a business in Israel: consumer-price and VAT rules, the anti-spam and privacy laws that govern lead follow-up and data uploads, Hebrew and multilingual search, account and billing settings, the Sunday-Thursday week, Shabbat and the holiday calendar, war-time demand shocks, the dominant local platforms, and the Google policies that name Israel. The most important idea: the most expensive mistakes in Israel are rarely about bids. They come from an ad or landing page price shown without VAT, a WhatsApp or SMS follow-up sent without recorded consent, or an ad that is in Hebrew but renders badly, schedules against the wrong week, or ignores a holiday that closes the business. **None of the legal entries here is legal or tax advice.** Each one names what to confirm with an Israeli lawyer or accountant.

Related entries elsewhere: language targeting removal and ad-language matching (PLT-STR-010), LSA not available in Israel (MKT-LOCAL-003), Google forwarding numbers available in Israel and call duration rules (MKT-LOCAL-014), local-services ad policy (Israel not named; MKT-LOCAL-015), Midrag and Easy as directories (MKT-LOCAL-016), financial services verification (MKT-NICHE-020), consent mode mechanics and EEA rules (MEA-CONV-017/018/019, MKT-WEB-021), enhanced conversions (MEA-CONV-010), price and promotion assets (PLT-ADS-018), editorial rules (PLT-ADS-029), misrepresentation and dishonest pricing (PLT-ADS-030), lead form assets (PLT-ADS-020), Keyword Planner volume limits (MKT-NICHE-001/002, PLT-KW-025), close variants and misspellings (PLT-KW-002, PLT-KW-015), brand lists matching Hebrew variants (PLT-KW-023), ad schedule as an on/off switch (PLT-STR-013), scheduling to capacity (PRC-DIAG-029), market shocks (PRC-DIAG-015, PRC-DIAG-040), seasonality adjustments (PLT-BID-023/024), audience list minimums and Customer Match (PLT-STR-015).

### MKT-ISR-001 · Prices offered to consumers must be the total price, including VAT, in ads and on landing pages
- **Claim:** Israel's Consumer Protection Law, 5741-1981, requires that the price of goods or services offered to a consumer be "the total price only" (section 17D), and the Consumer Protection and Fair Trade Authority treats a price shown without VAT as a breach. In November 2024 it notified the operator of Rami Levy stores of its intent to impose a NIS 843,597 sanction, one ground being price signage "not including VAT, contrary to section 17D" (a notice of intent, not a final decision). It fined Booking.com NIS 3 million in 2017 for listing room prices without VAT, and imposed a further NIS 2,560,800 sanction in September 2022 (under appeal as of ICPEN's report) citing sections 14C(a) and 17D; since July 2023 Booking.com shows Israeli IP visitors VAT-inclusive prices. A price is also a "material detail" under the misleading-practices section, 2(a). This is not legal advice.
- **Evidence type:** documented (regulator enforcement as reported by ICPEN and Israeli press; English translation of the original 1981 law, which predates sections 17B-17D); inference (application to search ads)
- **Confidence:** high (VAT-inclusive total price for consumers); medium (exact reach to every ad format, since we could not open the current Hebrew text of section 17D)
- **How it works:** The rule is about what the consumer will actually pay. Showing "₪1,000 + VAT" (+ מע"מ) to a consumer makes the shown price misleading, because the real price is higher. The Tax Authority's own guidance adds that when parties agree a price without saying whether VAT is included, the price is treated as including VAT (MKT-ISR-002). Consumers can sue (a law-firm update names class actions as a risk for price-display breaches), and the regulator can impose monetary sanctions. In the Booking.com case, the authority required "the total and final price, including the ... VAT" for Israeli consumers.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Check every place a price appears for a consumer business: RSA headlines and descriptions, price assets and promotion assets (PLT-ADS-018), image assets, lead-form text, the landing page, the Shopping feed (MKT-ISR-002) and the checkout. All must show the VAT-inclusive total, and they must agree, because Google's own misrepresentation policy also penalises prices that change between the ad and the page (PLT-ADS-030). "Price in the ad to filter clicks" (PRC-DIAG-031) works the same way in Israel, but the number must be the full consumer price. Ask the lawyer to confirm whether fees (delivery, installation, call-out charge) must be folded into the total or can be shown separately and how.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Business-to-business offers are normally priced before VAT, and section 17D protects "consumers". A B2B advertiser that shows ex-VAT prices should make the business audience unmistakable in both the ad and the page; confirm with a lawyer whether that is enough when the ad also reaches consumers. Tourists are exempt from VAT on some tourist services, which is why the Booking.com rule applied to Israeli consumers. Transactions inside the Eilat free-trade zone are described as VAT-exempt by a tax vendor guide (not an official source; confirm with an accountant).
- **Signals in data:** "+ מע"מ" or "לפני מע"מ" in consumer ad copy or price assets. A price in the ad lower than the price at checkout. Price assets not updated since before January 2025.
- **Sources:** [ICPEN: Booking.com to display total prices including VAT in Israel](https://icpen.org/news/1319) (checked 2026-10-13); [ice.co.il, 27/11/2024: Consumer Protection Authority sanction on Rami Levy, sections 17D, 17B, 2(a)(13)](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1039938) (checked 2026-10-13); [Globes, 2017-08-14: consumer watchdog fines Booking.com NIS 3m](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-consumer-watchdog-fines-bookingcom-nis-3m-1001201145) (checked 2026-10-13); [Barnea Jaffa Lande: price tagging remains mandatory (class actions and CPFTA fines)](https://barlaw.co.il/practice_areas/regulation/consumer-protection/client_updates/price-tagging-requirement-continues-to-be-a-mandatory-obligation/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Consumer Protection Law 5741-1981, English translation (WIPO Lex)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/128083) (checked 2026-10-13; original 1981 text, does not include the later sections 17B-17D); [Trykintsugi: Israel VAT guide 2026 (Eilat)](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/israel.md) (checked 2026-10-13; vendor). Not opened: the Knesset PDF of Amendment 11 (2002) to the Consumer Protection Law (redirected to a geo-maintenance page) and Hebrew Wikisource (blocked).
- **Open questions:** The exact current wording of sections 17B and 17D. Whether the law also requires prices to be shown in shekels for Israeli consumers (claimed by an academic clinic in 2019, without a cited section).

### MKT-ISR-002 · VAT is 18% since January 2025; Shopping feeds for Israel must include it
- **Claim:** Israel's standard VAT rate rose from 17% to 18% on 1 January 2025 **(changed 2025-01)**. The Tax Authority's interpretation directive 01/2025 (dated 5 December 2024) says goods are taxed by delivery date and services, as a general rule, by payment date, so a service paid from 1 January 2025 carries 18% even if agreed earlier (exceptions: accrual-basis businesses, related-party and non-cash deals, where the date of provision governs). Google Merchant Center names Israel among the countries where the product price "including any value-added tax (VAT)" must be submitted, in ILS, and it must match the landing page. Shopping ads and free listings are available in Israel, and the Shopping tab is shown to Israeli users.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** A 1-point VAT change moves every VAT-inclusive consumer price by about 0.85%. Any hard-coded price in ad text, price assets, promotion assets or feed rules that was correct in 2024 was wrong from January 2025. Merchant Center checks feed prices against the landing page, and a mismatch disapproves the item.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** In an audit of an Israeli account, look for prices written before 2025 in ad text and assets. For e-commerce, set the feed price to the VAT-inclusive consumer price and the currency to ILS. When planning margins or break-even CPC (MKT-NICHE-023), work on revenue *net* of VAT. Revenue reported to Google as conversion value may be VAT-inclusive; decide which, and keep it consistent with the client's CRM or accounting figures (MEA-CONV-024).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A press summary of the 2026 tax changes reported no new VAT change (checked 2026-10-13), but rates can change with a budget law. Check before quoting a rate. Merchants selling only to businesses still face Merchant Center's tax-inclusive rule for Israel.
- **Signals in data:** Merchant Center price-mismatch disapprovals. Conversion values that don't match accounting figures by roughly 18%.
- **Sources:** [Israel Tax Authority, interpretation notice 01/2025: VAT rate rises from 17% to 18% (PDF, Hebrew)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/represent-info-051224-2/he/vat_represent-info-051224-2.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13); [Herzog Fox & Neeman: VAT updates for 2025](https://herzoglaw.co.il/en/news-and-insights/vat-updates-for-2025-vat-rate-increase-israel-invoices-reform-and-the-economic-plan-bill/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Times of Israel, 2026-01-01: wave of price rises and tax hikes in 2026](https://www.timesofisrael.com/wave-of-price-rises-and-tax-hikes-takes-effect-fueling-costs-for-israelis-in-2026/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Price [price] – Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324371?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Shopping ads and free listings: supported countries – Merchant Center Help](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/160637?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-ISR-003 · The anti-spam law: SMS, email, WhatsApp and auto-dial marketing need prior consent, a "פרסומת" label and an opt-out
- **Claim:** Section 30A of the Communications (Telecommunications and Broadcasting) Law, in force since 1 December 2008, bans sending an "advertisement" by fax, automatic dialing system, SMS or electronic message without the recipient's prior express consent, given in writing, electronically or in a recorded call. The statute's own list is fax, automatic dialing system, SMS and "electronic message"; the Israeli Chamber of Commerce and a legal guide (Protocol) read "electronic message" as including WhatsApp, Messenger and Telegram, but Kol Zchut's page lists only fax, SMS, email and auto-dialer and no court ruling on WhatsApp was opened. Each message must start with the word "פרסומת" (in email, in the subject line), identify the sender, and offer a removal option through the same channel. Recipients can claim up to NIS 1,000 per message without proving damage; class actions are possible; sending without consent is also a criminal offence (Kol Zchut gives a fine of up to NIS 226,000 as of 2026). This is not legal advice.
- **Evidence type:** practitioner (secondary legal summaries: Kol Zchut, Chamber of Commerce, Meitar memo, Protocol), not the statute: the Knesset PDF of the 2008 amendment redirects to a maintenance page and Wikisource is blocked. Treat as "documented" only for the points on which all four agree (consent, NIS 1,000 per message, existing-customer exception).
- **Confidence:** medium-high (core rules); medium (WhatsApp coverage, which rests on two secondary sources' reading of "electronic message"; current fine amount, which only Kol Zchut gives)
- **How it works:** There is a narrow existing-customer exception: the person gave their details while buying or negotiating to buy, was told they would be used for marketing, was given a chance to refuse and did not, and the message concerns similar products or services. A business may also send one message to *another business* asking for consent. Each message is a separate claim, so a bulk send to a purchased or scraped list can create very large exposure. Officers and marketing managers can be personally liable.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Lead-generation funnels that end in SMS or WhatsApp nurture sequences. Add a separate, unticked consent checkbox for marketing messages to forms and lead form assets (PLT-ADS-020), and store the consent text, time and source with the lead. Treat "contact me about my request" consent and "send me marketing" consent as different things. Remarketing ads and Customer Match ads are not messages sent to the person, so this law is not the main constraint there; the privacy law is (MKT-ISR-004, MKT-ISR-005). Confirm with a lawyer: whether replying on WhatsApp to a person who started the chat from a message asset (MKT-ISR-013) is an "advertisement"; whether automated follow-ups after a quote count; and what consent wording is enough.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A genuinely transactional message (an appointment confirmation or answer to the person's question) is not "an advertisement" by definition, but adding a promotion to it can change that. A consent obtained by a lead seller does not automatically cover the buyer.
- **Signals in data:** Nurture sequences sent to every form lead. Purchased lists uploaded to SMS tools. Messages without "פרסומת" at the start. Complaints or letters demanding NIS 1,000 per message.
- **Sources:** [Kol Zchut: compensation for advertisements sent without consent (anti-spam law), updated 2026-02-17](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/פיצוי_בגין_משלוח_דברי_פרסומת_ללא_הסכמה_של_הנמען_(חוק_הספאם)) (checked 2026-10-13); [Federation of Israeli Chambers of Commerce: anti-spam law, refresher (2020-11-09)](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/115485/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Meitar: The Israeli anti-spam law (2008 memo)](https://meitar.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/83-israeli_anti-spam_law.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13); [Protocol: anti-spam law (2021)](https://protocol.co.il/spam-law/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Current criminal fine amounts (the 2008 memo gives NIS 202,000; Kol Zchut gives NIS 226,000 in 2026, consistent with indexation but not verified in the statute). Whether a court has ruled directly on WhatsApp Business replies started by the customer.

### MKT-ISR-004 · Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 (in force August 2025): what it means for leads, uploads and tracking
- **Claim:** Amendment 13 to the Privacy Protection Law, 5741-1981 took effect on 14 August 2025 **(changed 2025-08)**. It redefines personal information as data about an identified or identifiable person, explicitly listing location data and an "online identifier"; gives the Privacy Protection Authority (PPA) administrative fines that law firms describe as reaching "hundreds of thousands and even millions of NIS" in severe cases; lets courts award exemplary damages of up to NIS 10,000 without proof of harm; widens the notice that must be given when data is collected (purpose, recipients, consequences of refusing, rights); requires some organisations to appoint a data protection officer (DPO); and extends the limitation period for privacy claims arising after that date to seven years. It does **not** specifically regulate cookies or online advertising. This is not legal advice.
- **Evidence type:** practitioner (secondary legal summaries: Library of Congress legal summary for the effective date, NIS 10,000 damages, DPO list and seven-year limitation; IDI position paper for the "online identifier" and location examples in the definition; Meitar memo for the notice contents and the "hundreds of thousands and even millions of NIS" fine description; Arnon, Tadmor-Levy for the cookie point). Neither the statute nor the PPA's own guide was opened.
- **Confidence:** medium-high (effective date, damages, DPO, limitation period); medium (definition examples, notice list); low-medium (fine levels: only one law-firm memo describes them and gives no statutory figures)
- **How it works:** A lead form, a click ID stored with a lead, a hashed email list and a remarketing cookie all involve "personal information" under the new definition. Amendment 13 mostly strengthens enforcement and notices rather than adding new lawful bases for processing (IDI). DPO duties fall on bodies whose core activity is large-scale systematic monitoring or sensitive data (health funds, banks, insurers are named), and on data brokers, not on a typical small advertiser.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Before collecting leads or uploading Customer Match lists, check that the form or page tells people what the data is for, who receives it (including "service providers such as advertising platforms") and their rights; that the privacy policy says customer data is shared with third parties for services (a Google Customer Match requirement too, MKT-ISR-005); and that health, sexual, criminal, biometric or similar "special sensitivity" data is never used for audiences. Medical and clinic clients deserve extra care because health data is sensitive. Ask a lawyer to review the form notice text and whether Customer Match uploads need explicit consent for that client.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Practitioners sometimes say Amendment 13 "requires a cookie banner". The Arnon, Tadmor-Levy review says it does not specifically address cookies and the PPA has addressed cookie consent only for payment apps; a banner may still be prudent, but it is not a stated rule.
- **Signals in data:** Lead forms with no notice text. Customer Match lists built from data the client did not collect itself. Sensitive categories in audience names.
- **Sources:** [Library of Congress Global Legal Monitor, 2025-11-17: Israel amendment to Privacy Protection Law goes into effect](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2025-11-17/israel-amendment-to-privacy-protection-law-goes-into-effect/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Meitar: Amendment 13 is on the horizon (July 2025)](https://meitar.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Amendment-13-to-the-Privacy-Protection-Law-Is-on-the-Horizon-–-Is-Your-Organization-Ready-for-the-New-Era-in-the-World-of-Privacy.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13); [Pearl Cohen, 2025-09-03: significant amendment to the privacy law takes effect](https://www.pearlcohen.com/israel-significant-amendment-to-the-privacy-law-takes-effect/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Mondaq (Arnon, Tadmor-Levy): annual privacy review 2025, outlook for 2026](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1733296/annual-privacy-review-2025-outlook-for-2026) (checked 2026-10-13); [Israel Democracy Institute, March 2026: Reforming privacy law in Israel, assessing Amendment 13 (Hebrew PDF)](https://www.idi.org.il/media/30906/reforming-privacy-law-in-israel-assessing-amendment-13-and-what-it-still-fails-to-cover.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13). Not opened: the PPA professional guide to Amendment 13 on gov.il (only an index entry on Digital Policy Alert was reached).
- **Open questions:** The PPA's statutory fine amounts and how it treats online identifiers in advertising. Whether the PPA will issue cookie or tracking guidance.

### MKT-ISR-005 · Google's consent rules: the EU consent policy does not cover Israeli users, but Customer Match and EEA visitors still need care
- **Claim:** Google's EU User Consent Policy applies to "end users in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland", so Google does not require consent mode v2 signals for Israeli users. The Customer Match policy still requires first-party data only, consent "where required by law", and a privacy policy that discloses sharing customer data with third parties for services. Accounts need a good policy-compliance and payment history to use Customer Match.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high (Google scope); medium (what Israeli law requires for consent, see MKT-ISR-004)
- **How it works:** For Israeli traffic, Google tags can set ad cookies and run remarketing without consent-mode signals as far as Google's policy is concerned. Whether Israeli law requires consent is a separate question. Visitors from the EEA, UK or Switzerland (tourism, Jewish diaspora, B2B buyers) still fall under the EU policy, so a site that serves them needs consent mode for those users (MEA-CONV-019).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For a purely Israeli consumer site, do not assume a consent banner is needed for Google's sake, and do not assume it is forbidden either; follow the lawyer's view on Amendment 13. If the client sells abroad (tourism, e-commerce to Europe), set consent mode by region so that EU users are covered while Israeli measurement is not reduced. Before any Customer Match upload, confirm the data was collected by the client, the privacy policy has the third-party sharing line, and the account meets the eligibility rules.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A site-wide "basic" consent banner applied to Israeli users can cut observed conversions sharply with no legal requirement behind it (MEA-CONV-017). The reverse error is ignoring EU visitors in tourism accounts.
- **Signals in data:** Conversion drop after launching a consent banner. A large share of EU traffic in GA4 for an Israeli business with no regional consent settings.
- **Sources:** [EU user consent policy – Google](https://www.google.com/about/company/user-consent-policy/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Customer Match policy – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6299717?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About the customer matching process – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7474263?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-ISR-006 · Account setup: ILS and time zone are permanent, prepay is not offered in Israel, and VAT on Google's invoice needs an accountant
- **Claim:** A Google Ads account's currency and time zone are "permanently set" at creation and cannot be changed; a different currency or time zone needs a new account. Google lists Israel among the "Countries and territories where prepay isn't available", so Israeli accounts need another payment setting (automatic payments or, if eligible, monthly invoicing); the page does not confirm which of these are offered in Israel. Google's "Taxes in your country" page has no Israel section, so how VAT applies to Google Ads spend for an Israeli business is not documented there.
- **Evidence type:** documented (currency, time zone, prepay); open (VAT treatment)
- **Confidence:** high (settings and prepay); low (VAT treatment)
- **How it works:** Because reporting days and billing follow the account's time zone, an account created in a US time zone shifts every Israeli day boundary by hours. That distorts dayparting, ad schedules and daily budget pacing for a business whose customers live on Israel time. An account created in USD bills in USD and exposes the client to exchange-rate swings.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** When opening an account for an Israeli client, choose ILS and Israel's time zone at creation. When taking over an old account, check both; if they are wrong and the account is young, a new account may be cheaper than living with the error. Do not promise a prepaid budget cap through manual payments; use the account budget or campaign budgets instead. Ask the client's accountant how the Google Ads invoice is treated for VAT and expense purposes, and which Google entity bills them, before quoting "cost including VAT" in reports.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Manager accounts can have the time zone reset once, eastward only. Agencies with an MCC in another time zone can still create client accounts in Israel time.
- **Signals in data:** Hour-of-day reports in which the "night" falls during Israeli business hours. Invoices in USD. A client asking to "top up" a prepaid balance.
- **Sources:** [About your language, number format, time zone, and currency settings – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9842104?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Know your billing options – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1704418?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Taxes in your country – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375370?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13; no Israel section in the content returned); [Globes, 2018-05-10: Tax Authority can't make Google pay VAT in Israel](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-tax-authority-cant-make-google-pay-vat-in-israel-1001235564) (checked 2026-10-13; historical). A vendor tax guide says foreign digital services to Israeli businesses fall under a reverse charge ([Trykintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/middle-east/israel.md), checked 2026-10-13); not confirmed by any official source opened.
- **Open questions:** Which Google entity contracts with Israeli advertisers today, whether Google charges Israeli VAT, and whether the business must self-assess it. The Google Ads Terms page for Israel could not be opened (server error).

### MKT-ISR-007 · Hebrew ad text is right-to-left: mixed English, numbers and punctuation can render in the wrong place
- **Claim:** Hebrew is a supported Google Ads language, but Hebrew ads mix scripts. Under the Unicode bidirectional algorithm, punctuation and spaces between right-to-left and left-to-right text take the direction of the surrounding base text, so marks such as "!", "?", "-", "/" or "%" next to an English brand name or a number can display at the wrong end. Numbers keep left-to-right order inside right-to-left text.
- **Evidence type:** documented (language support; bidi rules); inference (effect on Google Ads rendering)
- **Confidence:** high (the bidi mechanism); medium (how often Google's ad renderer shows the problem; we did not test)
- **How it works:** A headline such as an English brand name followed by "!" or a phone number with a hyphen can come out reordered when the ad is shown, depending on the base direction the surface applies. The editor in Google Ads may show it one way and the SERP another.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Preview every Hebrew ad on a phone and in the ad preview tool before approving it. Avoid ending a Hebrew headline with a Latin word followed by punctuation. Put prices as "₪199" or "199 ₪" consistently and test which reads correctly. Keep ranges ("24/7", "1-3") away from line edges. Write Hebrew, not machine translation from English; check AI-generated or "text customization" assets for wrong-gender or literal translations (PLT-ADS-025). Ad text must also be in a language Google supports for the ad, or the ad is disapproved.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Pure Hebrew text without Latin words or symbols rarely has the problem. Google's editorial rules on punctuation and capitalisation still apply (PLT-ADS-029).
- **Signals in data:** Screenshots from clients showing reversed punctuation. Lower CTR on one ad variant that differs only in mixed-script headlines.
- **Sources:** [About available languages in the Google Ads interface – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6333734?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [W3C Internationalization: Unicode bidirectional algorithm basics](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/uba-basics) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether Google sets an explicit right-to-left base direction for Hebrew ads on every surface (Search, AI Overviews, Maps, Display).

### MKT-ISR-008 · Hebrew keywords: attached prefixes, two spelling systems, English-Hebrew brand forms and wrong-keyboard queries
- **Claim:** Hebrew attaches one-letter prefixes for "the", "and", "in", "to", "from", "as" and "that" directly to words (ה, ו, ב, ל, מ, כ, ש), so one concept appears in many surface forms. Everyday Hebrew uses ktiv male (full spelling) as the dominant system, but writers also mix in shorter forms, so the same word can be spelled more than one way. Israelis also search brands in both Latin and Hebrew letters. Google holds a patent on detecting a query typed in the wrong keyboard layout (its main example is "news" typed on a Hebrew layout) and correcting it.
- **Evidence type:** documented (language facts; Google's brand-list matching covers Hebrew variants; patent); hypothesis (that Google Ads applies keyboard-layout correction to matching today)
- **Confidence:** high (language facts); medium (how far close variants cover prefixes and spelling); low (keyboard-layout correction in ad matching)
- **How it works:** Exact and phrase match include close variants such as misspellings and inflections (PLT-KW-002), and search terms may be reported in corrected form (PLT-KW-015). Brand lists match brand entities across scripts; Google's example includes Hebrew variants of "YouTube" (PLT-KW-023). Israel's whole market is about 10 million people (MKT-ISR-009), and many Hebrew queries are rare, so Keyword Planner often shows low or zero volumes (MKT-NICHE-002).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Build keyword lists from real phrasing: the client's own search terms, autocomplete and competitors' pages, not from translated English lists. Include both the Hebrew and the Latin form of brand names and product models. Check the search terms report for prefixed and alternative spellings before adding negatives, because a negative keyword does not cover close variants in the same way (PLT-KW-018). Expect Keyword Planner to undercount; treat small numbers as "some demand" rather than measurements. Use broad or phrase match with Smart Bidding to catch spelling variety when conversion tracking is solid.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Some prefixes change meaning ("מ" = "from" can turn a buying query into a different one). Hebrew-English homographs and short acronyms can match unrelated queries.
- **Signals in data:** Many low-volume search terms that are spelling or prefix variants of one keyword. Brand searches in Latin letters converting differently from Hebrew ones. Gibberish-looking Latin search terms that map to Hebrew words on a Hebrew keyboard.
- **Sources:** [Prefixes in Hebrew – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefixes_in_Hebrew) (checked 2026-10-13); [Hebrew spelling – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_spelling) (checked 2026-10-13); [US8676824B2: Automatic search query correction – Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8676824) (checked 2026-10-13); brand-list matching via PLT-KW-023.
- **Open questions:** Whether close variants reliably match prefixed forms (e.g. a keyword without "ה" and a query with it). Whether keyboard-layout correction is used in ad matching.

### MKT-ISR-009 · A multilingual market: Arabic and Russian speakers need their own ads now that ad language is the targeting
- **Claim:** About 21% of Israel's population is Arab (CBS figures reported in 2023 and 2024), and many Israelis are Russian-speaking immigrants. In a 2013 CBS survey reported by Ynet, Hebrew was the mother tongue of 49% of Israelis over 20, Arabic of 18% and Russian of 15%; 17% of Arabs did not read Hebrew at all and 39% of Russian-speaking immigrants did not read it or read it poorly. From September 2026 Google removes campaign-level language targeting from Search and Performance Max (announced by Google's Ads Liaison on 14 August 2026, as reported by trade press); ads are matched by the language of the ad and landing page, the search term's language and the user's language settings, with ads matching the search term's language prioritised (PLT-STR-010) **(changed 2026-09)**.
- **Evidence type:** documented (population, CBS via Ynet); google-statement (language-targeting change, Ads Liaison post reported by trade press; Google's language-support page opened does not yet mention it); study (2013 self-reported survey, old)
- **Confidence:** high (population share); low-medium (language proficiency today, since the survey is from 2013)
- **How it works:** With language targeting gone, an Arabic or Russian ad group is reached by queries and users in that language. A Hebrew-only account will mostly not be shown for Arabic or Russian queries, and an Arabic ad can still serve on a Hebrew query if Google judges it the best match. Arabic is right-to-left (MKT-ISR-007); Russian is left-to-right Cyrillic.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For businesses serving Arab towns or Russian-speaking customers (medical, legal, real estate, insurance, home services in specific cities), create separate ad groups or campaigns with ads, assets and landing pages in that language, and check the search terms report for language mixing. Staff the phone or WhatsApp line with someone who speaks the language; otherwise the leads are wasted. Do not translate Hebrew ads by machine for these audiences.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Many Arab and Russian-speaking Israelis search in Hebrew or English for many topics; a separate language campaign may get little volume. Geography (Arab towns, cities with large immigrant populations) is often a better first split than language.
- **Signals in data:** Arabic or Cyrillic search terms in a Hebrew campaign. Calls from customers who cannot be served in their language.
- **Sources:** [Ynet, 2023-09-13: population on eve of Rosh Hashanah (CBS)](https://www.ynetnews.com/magazine/article/s100zdfjkp) (checked 2026-10-13); [Ynet, 2024-09-25: population nears 10 million (CBS)](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/b1y0nwwca) (checked 2026-10-13); [Ynet, 2013-01-21: CBS, 27% of Israelis struggle with Hebrew](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/4335235) (checked 2026-10-13); [About available languages in the Google Ads interface](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6333734?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13; Hebrew and Arabic supported for ads language targeting); [PPC Land, 2026-08-14: Google Ads drops language targeting in September](https://ppc.land/google-ads-drops-language-targeting-in-september-nine-months-past-deadline/) (checked 2026-10-13; trade report of the Ads Liaison announcement)
- **Open questions:** Current share of searches in Arabic and Russian in Israel. Recent CBS language data (the newest opened is from 2013).

### MKT-ISR-010 · The week runs Sunday to Thursday, and Shabbat moves with sunset, which a fixed ad schedule cannot follow
- **Claim:** Israeli business hours typically run Sunday to Thursday (for example, Yad2's customer service: Sunday-Thursday 08:30-16:00), with Friday short and Shabbat from Friday sunset to Saturday nightfall. Shabbat start in Tel Aviv moves through the year: candle-lighting was 16:19 on Friday 18 December 2026 and 19:30 on Friday 25 June 2027. A Google Ads ad schedule is fixed by day and clock time (PLT-STR-013), so it cannot follow sunset by itself. Many businesses close on Shabbat; online stores usually stay open, and a fashion site that closed on Shabbat in August 2026 drew both praise and boycott threats.
- **Evidence type:** documented (Shabbat times; Yad2 hours; press); practitioner (business patterns); inference (scheduling approach)
- **Confidence:** high (times); medium (general business patterns)
- **How it works:** For businesses that close for Shabbat, ads that run after the phones stop produce unanswered calls and messages that go cold over 25 hours. For businesses that stay open or sell online, Saturday evening after Shabbat can be busy. Clients who observe Shabbat may object to their ads showing on Shabbat at all, as a matter of principle.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Ask every client three questions: do you answer calls on Friday afternoon, on Shabbat, and on Saturday night? If the answer is no and the client wants no ads on Shabbat, set the Friday cut-off to the earliest winter candle-lighting time, or change the schedule seasonally (about every month, or with a script or automated rule), and restart on Saturday night after the latest summer nightfall. Treat Sunday as a full working day in plans and reports, and compare weeks Sunday to Saturday. Schedule call assets to staffed hours (PRC-DIAG-029, MKT-LOCAL-014).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** (practitioner) Muslim-owned businesses may close on Friday and Christian-owned ones on Sunday instead. Emergency services (locksmith, towing, plumbing) in secular areas may work on Shabbat. Turning ads off for Shabbat under Smart Bidding also changes budget pacing on the remaining days (PLT-STR-021).
- **Signals in data:** Conversion-rate dips on Friday afternoon and Saturday daytime; spikes on Saturday night and Sunday morning; missed calls clustered on Friday afternoon.
- **Sources:** [Hebcal Shabbat times, Tel Aviv, 18 Dec 2026](https://www.hebcal.com/shabbat?cfg=json&geonameid=293397&gy=2026&gm=12&gd=18&M=on) (checked 2026-10-13); [Hebcal Shabbat times, Tel Aviv, 25 Jun 2027](https://www.hebcal.com/shabbat?cfg=json&geonameid=293397&gy=2027&gm=6&gd=25&M=on) (checked 2026-10-13); [Yad2 home page](https://www.yad2.co.il/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Ynet, 2026-08-29: pop star shuts fashion site for Shabbat](https://www.ynetnews.com/business/article/sk8escldgl) (checked 2026-10-13). Not opened: Kol Zchut's English page on weekly rest (English content removed in 2023).
- **Open questions:** Measured search demand by hour across Shabbat, by category. No dataset was opened.

### MKT-ISR-011 · The holiday calendar: Tishrei, Pesach, election day and Black Friday shape demand and opening days
- **Claim:** Jewish holidays follow the lunar calendar, so their Gregorian dates move each year. In Israel for 2026-2027: Rosh Hashana 11-13 Sep 2026; Yom Kippur 20-21 Sep; Sukkot 25 Sep-2 Oct; Shmini Atzeret/Simchat Torah 2-3 Oct; Chanukah 4-12 Dec 2026; Purim 22-23 Mar 2027; Pesach 21-28 Apr 2027; Yom HaZikaron and Yom HaAtzma'ut 10-12 May; Shavuot 10-11 Jun 2027 (sunset to nightfall). The Knesset election on **27 October 2026** (confirmed by the coalition in July 2026 as the originally scheduled date) is a paid day off for most workers, except "services to the public" listed by the Central Elections Committee. Black Friday has become large: card spending on Black Friday 2025 exceeded NIS 1.5 billion, up 28% on 2024, with online transactions up 38%.
- **Evidence type:** documented (dates; election day; card data reported from Shva); practitioner (category effects)
- **Confidence:** high (dates); medium (effects on demand, which vary by category and were not measured here)
- **How it works:** Holiday eves and first and last days behave like Shabbat (businesses closed, many people offline); the intermediate days of Sukkot and Pesach are part-holiday with schools closed. The September-October cluster interrupts work for about four weeks, so B2B and many services slow down, and demand often builds just after "אחרי החגים" (after the holidays). Before Pesach, demand rises for cleaning, home goods, food and travel; before Rosh Hashana, gifts and food.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Put the year's holidays in the account plan and annotations, and compare like with like (PRC-DIAG-014): compare Sukkot to last year's Sukkot, not to the same calendar dates. Pause or cut ads for businesses that are closed, and plan budget and offers for the pre-holiday rush. On Yom Kippur almost everything stops; many advertisers pause. Use seasonality adjustments only for short, unusual conversion-rate changes the bidder cannot know about, not for regular holidays (PLT-BID-023/024). Default ad schedule: Sunday to Thursday plus Friday morning; pause or lower bids over Shabbat and holiday eves unless the business answers then, and name Chanukah when a plan's window includes December (practitioner).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Arab and Christian customers follow other calendars (Ramadan and Eid, Christmas). Tourism and leisure peak during the holidays rather than drop.
- **Signals in data:** Year-on-year comparisons that look like crashes because the holidays fell in different months. Conversion lag after the holidays as people return to tasks they postponed.
- **Sources:** [Hebcal: Jewish holidays 2026-2027 (Israel schedule)](https://www.hebcal.com/holidays/2026-2027?i=on) (checked 2026-10-13); [Israel Hayom, 2026-07-12: Israel's next official election date set](https://www.israelhayom.com/2026/07/12/israels-next-official-election-date-set) (checked 2026-10-13); [Nefesh B'Nefesh: the Israeli elections (paid holiday)](https://www.nbn.org.il/life-in-israel/employment/the-israeli-elections/) (checked 2026-10-13); [JNS via Columbus Jewish News, 2025-12-02: Israelis spent more this Black Friday than before the war (Shva data)](https://www.columbusjewishnews.com/jns/israelis-spent-more-this-black-friday-than-before-the-war/article_eec0fe52-588b-5e45-b755-97ea5e0bc3a0.html) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-ISR-012 · War and emergency periods cut consumer demand sharply and suddenly; plan for them like outages, not seasons
- **Claim:** Israel's recent wars produced measurable consumption shocks. After the June 2025 Iran war, including a roughly two-week economic shutdown, GDP shrank at an annualised 3.5% in Q2 2025 (CBS initial estimate) and private consumption fell 4.1% (basis not stated in the report). The war with Iran in March-April 2026 left Q1 2026 GDP down an annualised 3.3% in the CBS first estimate, revised in June 2026 to 3.8%, with private consumption down 4.7% annualised in the first estimate and 5% in the revision. Demand bounced back afterwards: 2025 Black Friday spending exceeded the pre-war 2022 level (MKT-ISR-011).
- **Evidence type:** documented (CBS figures as reported by Globes); practitioner (campaign handling)
- **Confidence:** high (that demand drops); medium (category-level effects, which are not in these figures)
- **How it works:** During missile alerts and Home Front Command restrictions, people stay near shelters, businesses close or cannot send technicians, and attention moves to news. Practitioners expect some categories to rise (for example generators, safe-room equipment, delivery) while most fall; the GDP figures do not break this down. Smart Bidding learns from the sudden drop in conversion rate and can over-correct when demand returns.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Agree an emergency plan with the client in advance: which campaigns pause, who decides, and how the ads should read (tone-deaf copy during a war harms the brand). If the business cannot serve customers, pause rather than let the bidder learn from a collapsed conversion rate. Mark the period in annotations and exclude it from comparisons (PRC-DIAG-015, PRC-DIAG-040). If conversion tracking broke (e.g. the call centre closed), consider data exclusions (MEA-CONV-022). On restart, watch for a pent-up rebound.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Businesses serving customers outside Israel, or online-only sellers, may see little effect. Regional closures (north or south only) call for geo pauses, not account pauses.
- **Signals in data:** Abrupt nationwide drops in impressions and conversion rate on the day of an escalation, across all campaigns; matched-location shifts.
- **Sources:** [Globes, 2025-08-17: Iran war depresses Israeli economy](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001519148) (checked 2026-10-13); [Globes, 2026-05-17: Iran campaign depresses Israel's economy](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001543147) (checked 2026-10-13); [Globes, 2026-06-16: Revised Q1 figures show sharper Israeli GDP contraction](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001546024) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-ISR-013 · Google is almost the whole search market and WhatsApp is almost universal: put WhatsApp in the ad and measure it
- **Claim:** StatCounter put Google at 98.38% of search-engine use in Israel in September 2026 (Bing 1.13%). An Israel Internet Association figure quoted by Globes says 98% of Israelis use WhatsApp, and 95% of users use it daily (2024). Google Ads message assets let a Search or Performance Max ad open a chat on WhatsApp (or SMS and other apps). They are in beta, need a WhatsApp Business number, completed advertiser verification, an eligible vertical and a good policy history. A trade site reported in October 2024, citing a practitioner's LinkedIn post rather than Google, that the WhatsApp asset's country picker expanded to 233 countries including Israel; Google's own page does not list countries, and the feature is still described as beta.
- **Evidence type:** documented (Google requirements; StatCounter); study (survey figure via press); practitioner (country list, from a LinkedIn post relayed by a trade site)
- **Confidence:** high (Google's search share and the asset's requirements); medium (WhatsApp usage figure); low (Israel availability: unconfirmed by Google, beta, and the only source is second-hand)
- **How it works:** Many Israelis prefer to message a business rather than call or fill in a form. The message asset adds a chat button; the chat opens on the user's phone. Google's Help page advises choosing "Conversions" as the bid goal and links to a separate reporting guide; how a chat start becomes a conversion depends on setup. A click-to-WhatsApp link on the landing page is the alternative where the asset is not available.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For local services and lead businesses in Israel, offer WhatsApp as a contact route if someone can answer quickly. Track WhatsApp clicks on the landing page as a conversion, and value them by how many become customers, since a chat start is a weak signal (like a short call, MEA-CONV-023). Follow-up marketing by WhatsApp is subject to the anti-spam law (MKT-ISR-003). With Google at about 98%, Microsoft Advertising adds little volume in Israel; a test is optional (PRC-DIAG-034).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Sensitive verticals (for example campaigns and elections) are not eligible for message assets. New advertisers may see limited impressions at first. Counting every WhatsApp click as a lead inflates results.
- **Signals in data:** Many WhatsApp-link clicks with few booked jobs. Calls falling as WhatsApp clicks rise after adding the button.
- **Sources:** [StatCounter: search engine market share Israel](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/israel) (checked 2026-10-13); [Globes, 2024-07-11: WhatsApp ranked Israel's top 2024 brand](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001484162) (checked 2026-10-13); [About message assets – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14888522?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC News Feed, 2024-10-31: WhatsApp extension now in 233 countries](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2024-10/whatsapp-extension-now-available-in-233-countries-europe-included/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Search Engine Land: WhatsApp in Google Ads, message assets](https://searchengineland.com/?p=450630) (checked 2026-10-13; undated, names no countries)
- **Open questions:** Whether message assets are actually offered to Israeli accounts (check in the account before promising it to a client), and whether they are still beta or generally available. How Google counts message conversions by default.

### MKT-ISR-017 · Medical and clinic advertising: Israeli law binds the doctor and anyone advertising for them; edited model images need a label
- **Claim:** Section 11 of the Physicians Ordinance [New Version], 1976 bars a licensed physician from advertising their practice in a way that could mislead or that harms the dignity of the profession, lets the Minister of Health ban further types of advertising, applies the same limits to *anyone else* advertising a physician's practice, and makes the physician responsible for taking all reasonable steps to stop ads others run for them. The Ministry of Health's 2022 dental advertising position paper rejects exaggerated, misleading, comparative or superlative claims, fear-inducing content and unrealistic expectations of treatment. Separately, the "Photoshop law" (passed 2012, in force 1 January 2013) requires a commercial ad that uses editing to narrow a model's body to say so clearly, on at least 7% of the ad's area; it also requires a doctor's certificate that the model is not underweight (BMI under 18.5 for adults). Ads whose content and distribution show they are not aimed at the Israeli public are exempt. This is not legal advice.
- **Evidence type:** documented (statute text on gov.il; Ministry of Health paper; Chamber of Commerce summary)
- **Confidence:** high (the ordinance); medium (detailed prohibited categories, which sit in regulations we did not open)
- **How it works:** An agency writing ads for a clinic falls under section 11(d), and the doctor carries the risk under 11(e)-(f). Google's own policies add their own limits for health care (MKT-ISR-016, PLT-ADS-030).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For doctors, dentists and aesthetic clinics, avoid "the best", "guaranteed", fear messages and before-after promises in ads and on landing pages; get the client to approve copy in writing and to check it against the profession's advertising regulations. For fashion and beauty image assets, ask whether model images were edited to look thinner, and add the required label if so.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The detailed physician and dentist advertising regulations (2008/2009 "prohibited advertising" regulations) were not opened; testimonials, prices and discounts may be addressed there. Health data in audiences is also a privacy risk (MKT-ISR-004).
- **Signals in data:** Superlative claims in clinic ads; Google health-care disapprovals; competitor clinics with very cautious copy (a sign of local enforcement).
- **Sources:** [Physicians Ordinance [New Version] 5737-1976, consolidated text (gov.il PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/rofim01/ru/files_legislation_rofim_Rofim_01.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13); [Ministry of Health, Division of Dental Health: advertising in dentistry, position paper, 2022-02-15 (Hebrew PDF)](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/policy/dent-184399522/he/files_regulation_dental_health_dent_184399522.pdf) (checked 2026-10-13); [Federation of Israeli Chambers of Commerce: the "Photoshop law", 2013](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/1857/2286/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Contents of the Physicians (Prohibited Advertising) Regulations 2008 and the dentists' 2007/2009 regulations: testimonials, prices, before-after photos.

## Common mistakes
- Showing consumer prices "+ מע"מ" in ads, price assets or landing pages, or leaving 17%-era prices in place after January 2025.
- Sending SMS or WhatsApp marketing to every form lead without a separate, recorded marketing consent and without "פרסומת" at the start of each message.
- Uploading Customer Match lists bought from or shared by third parties, or using health data in audiences.
- Adding a blocking consent banner for Israeli traffic "because Google requires it" (it does not for Israel), or forgetting EU visitors in tourism and export accounts.
- Creating the account in USD or in a US time zone; promising a prepaid budget in Israel.
- Translating English keyword lists into Hebrew, ignoring prefixed and alternative spellings, and treating zero Keyword Planner volume as "no demand".
- Mixed-script Hebrew headlines that render with punctuation in the wrong place, approved without a phone preview.
- Running Hebrew-only ads for Arabic- or Russian-speaking customers after the 2026 language-targeting change, or running Arabic or Russian ads with no one to answer in that language.
- Treating Friday as a full working day, or a fixed Friday 18:00 cut-off all year when Shabbat starts between about 16:00 and 19:30.
- Comparing this year's September or April with last year's without lining up the holidays, the election day or war periods.
- Counting every WhatsApp click as a lead.
- Running gambling ads for an unlicensed operator, or prescription drug names on a pharmacy landing page.
