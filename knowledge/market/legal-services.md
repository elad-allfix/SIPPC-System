# Legal services: bar advertising rules

Law-firm advertising is regulated by each state's bar, on top of Google's policies. The rules decide what ad copy may say, whether each ad must be filed before or after it runs, and what that costs. Check the state's rules before writing copy or a launch date. This file starts with Texas; other states are a known gap (research-gaps.md).

Related entries elsewhere: Local Services Ads ranking and verification (MKT-LOCAL-002, MKT-LOCAL-019), language matching for Spanish campaigns (PLT-STR-010), ad claims that need proof (PLT-ADS-029, PLT-ADS-030).

### MKT-LEGAL-001 · Texas: non-exempt lawyer ads are filed with the State Bar within 10 days, $100 each
- **Claim:** Under Texas Disciplinary Rule 7.04(a), a lawyer files an advertisement "no later than ten (10) days after the date of dissemination", with a copy of the ad as it appears, an application and a fee. The State Bar's submission page sets the fee at $100 per advertisement, and each advertisement needs a separate application packet. Optional pre-approval (Rule 7.04(c)) needs submission at least 30 days before first dissemination.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high for the deadline and pre-approval timing (rule text); medium for the fee (State Bar page, can change)
- **How it works:** For internet ads the Bar asks for a print-out of the homepage or banner ad plus the website it links to; a previously approved website can be cited by file number. The page does not address search text ads specifically.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** A Texas law firm's 90-day plan budgets $100 per non-exempt ad and a filing step within 10 days of launch; with many ad variants, the filing cost and work push toward fewer, stable ads. If unsure whether Search text ads are treated as one ad or many, the firm asks the Bar's Advertising Review Department before launch.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Exempt communications (MKT-LEGAL-002) need no filing but must still comply with Rules 7.01-7.03. Fees and procedures are set by the Bar and change; recheck before quoting.
- **Signals in data:** none in Google Ads; this is a compliance step outside the account.
- **Sources:** [Rule 7.04 – Texas Center for Legal Ethics](https://www.legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/filing-requirements-for-advertisements-and-solicitation-communications/) (checked 2026-10-14); [Mandatory Submission Steps – State Bar of Texas](https://www.texasbar.com/Content/NavigationMenu/ForLawyers/MembershipInformation/AdvertisingReview2/SubSteps.htm) (checked 2026-10-14)

### MKT-LEGAL-002 · Texas: website pages except the homepage are exempt; "no fee unless we win" copy is not on the exempt list
- **Claim:** Rule 7.05(b) exempts information and links on a law firm website "except the contents of the website homepage". Rule 7.05(i) exempts communications that contain only listed information: names, addresses, phone numbers, service hours, practice areas, bar admissions, education, licences, foreign language abilities, Texas Board of Legal Specialization certification, fees "for an initial consultation or routine legal services", and a few more. Contingent-fee wording such as "no fee unless we win" is not on that list.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high for the rule text; medium for applying it to a specific ad (the Bar decides)
- **How it works:** An ad with only exempt items needs no filing. An ad that adds a contingent-fee promise, results or claims of quality is very likely non-exempt and goes through MKT-LEGAL-001. New landing pages (not the homepage) are exempt but must still comply with Rules 7.01 and 7.02 (for example, "specialist" only with TBLS certification).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Decide ad copy and filing together: either keep ads to exempt information, or plan the filing for each ad that uses fee or outcome claims. Send ad clicks to landing pages rather than the homepage where possible; the homepage is the one website page that needs filing unless its content is exempt.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Whether a fee disclosure required by the Rules (item 12) covers a particular contingent-fee statement is not settled on this page; ask the Bar.
- **Signals in data:** competitor copy using "no fee unless we win" says nothing about whether they filed.
- **Sources:** [Rule 7.05 – Texas Center for Legal Ethics](https://legalethicstexas.com/resources/rules/texas-disciplinary-rules-of-professional-conduct/communications-exempt-from-filing-requirements/) (checked 2026-10-14)
