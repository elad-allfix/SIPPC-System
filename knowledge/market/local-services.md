# Local service businesses: channels, rules and call handling

What a strategist needs to plan Google advertising for a local service business (plumber, locksmith, groomer, cleaner): Local Services Ads (pay per lead), Google Business Profile and the local pack (free, ranked by relevance, distance and prominence), location assets and Maps ads in Google Ads, call measurement, local-services ad policy by country, and lead marketplaces. The single most important idea: for these businesses the constraint is usually **operational, not media**. The phone has to be answered, the job has to be inside a drivable area, and the listing has to survive verification. Since 2026-10, Local Services Ads also charge for missed calls during business hours. Check eligibility per country and per category before planning a channel. These rules changed a great deal in 2025-2026.

Related entries elsewhere: call assets and number verification (PLT-ADS-019), call conversion types and forwarding numbers (MEA-CONV-023, MKT-WEB-024), call-only ad retirement (PRC-DIAG-030), scheduling to capacity (PRC-DIAG-029), geo tiering (PRC-DIAG-032), radius and location options (PLT-STR-006, PLT-STR-009), ad schedule under Smart Bidding (PLT-STR-013), speed to lead (MKT-WEB-025), marketplace prices as value benchmarks (MKT-NICHE-026), break-even CPC (MKT-NICHE-023).

### MKT-LOCAL-001 · Local Services Ads charge per lead, and since October 2026 that includes missed calls
- **Claim:** Local Services Ads (LSA) charge per valid lead, not per click. Google defines charged leads as: a call that is answered or called back; a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds; a call that reaches voicemail or the greeting where the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds, whether or not a message is left; a text or email message; and a booking request. Follow-up contacts from the same phone number or email within 15 days of the last interaction are not charged again. The missed-call charge took effect on 2026-10-01 **(changed 2026-10)**.
- **Evidence type:** documented; google-statement (effective date, from Google's advertiser email as reported)
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** "Business hours" means the hours in the Google Business Profile. If the ad schedule differs, unanswered calls are charged only when the ad schedule and business hours overlap. With a phone menu, the 20-second timer resets at the first key press, and a caller who hangs up without pressing a key is not charged. Without a menu, Google has not said whether the timer starts at connection or first ring (press coverage notes this gap). Google's advertiser email also says that when the first call was *not* chargeable, a later call between the same parties that meets the valid-lead criteria can be charged. Google says message leads are "typically priced lower than phone leads". Leads judged invalid, spam or duplicate at first assessment are not charged, and Google keeps assessing lead quality for 30 days (MKT-LOCAL-005). Booking leads exist only in the US and Canada.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Since October 2026 an unanswered phone is a direct cost on LSA, as well as lost revenue. Before turning LSA on, check the GBP hours against the hours someone actually answers the phone. Trim the hours or the ad schedule to staffed time (MKT-LOCAL-017). Turn on message leads if staff can reply quickly. Compare LSA on cost per *booked job*, not cost per lead (MKT-LOCAL-016).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A voicemail greeting that plays for 20 seconds produces a charged lead even if the caller leaves no message. An IVR that asks callers to "press 1" protects you from hang-ups, but it adds friction. GBP hours of "open 24 hours" with no night cover produce charged missed calls at night.
- **Signals in data:** Charged leads with no recording or a very short recording. Missed-call leads clustered at lunch or at the start and end of the day. Charged lead count higher than the CRM's count of real enquiries.
- **Sources:** [How charges work for Local Services Ads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7195435) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google to charge for missed & subsequent calls on LSAs – Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-lsa-missed-subsequent-calls-41940.html) (checked 2026-10-13); [Invoca packages AI agents as Google bills missed LSA calls over 20 seconds – PPC Land](https://ppc.land/invoca-packages-ai-agents-as-google-bills-missed-lsa-calls-over-20-seconds/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether the missed-call rule applies outside the US. The Help page does not limit it by country, but the press coverage is US-focused and neither press article states a geographic scope (fact-checked 2026-10-13).

### MKT-LOCAL-002 · LSA ranking: bid, likelihood of a lead, and profile quality
- **Claim:** Google ranks LSA listings in an auction on "bid and overall profile quality". The documented inputs are: the bid (a maximum per lead; you may pay less); likelihood of a lead (responsiveness, since "Missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness"; search context, meaning the job searched, the time and the location; relevance; and contact options, since enabling message and booking leads adds ways to reach you); and profile quality (rating, number of reviews, average response time, high-quality images, verification checks completed). Google says higher-quality profiles "may rank higher and may also pay lower costs per lead".
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high (the list of factors); low (their weights, which are not published)
- **How it works:** The searcher's location enters as "search context", so proximity still matters, but the current page does not list proximity, business hours or complaints as separate factors. Older versions of the page, still widely quoted by practitioners, named proximity, review score and count, responsiveness, business hours and serious or repeated complaints. We did not open an archived copy, so treat that list as historical. Ranking also "optimizes for the overall health and diversity" of the LSA ecosystem. A business with several locations covering the same area shows only its highest-ranked ad for a given query.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** The levers a small business controls are answering every call (MKT-LOCAL-017), replying to messages quickly, collecting genuine reviews (within policy, MKT-LOCAL-012), uploading real photos, and completing verification. Raising the bid works only on top of these.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** In thin markets with few advertisers, a low-quality profile can still get leads. In dense markets a high bid cannot make up for a poor response record. The "diversity" objective means a top profile will not win every impression.
- **Signals in data:** Lead volume drops after a run of missed calls. Impressions or leads rise after reviews and photos are added.
- **Sources:** [How Local Services Ads ranking works – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Getting started with Local Services Ads – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13). The ranking page support.google.com/localservices/answer/7189591 could not be opened on 2026-10-13.
- **Open questions:** When the wording changed from the older proximity/hours/complaints list.

### MKT-LOCAL-003 · LSA eligibility: check country and category per business, because absence from a list proves nothing
- **Claim:** LSA is available "only for certain service categories" and in a limited set of countries. The US-English "Getting started" page we opened lists these countries: Austria, Belgium, Canada, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, United Kingdom and United States. **Israel is not listed.** Its US category list includes many home services: locksmiths, garage door services, plumbers, HVAC, electricians, pest control, house cleaning, carpet cleaning, appliance repair, movers, roofers, water damage and others. Some categories are limited to California and Florida, for example drain experts and home insulation. "Pet grooming" also appears on that page.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** medium (the lists change, and our reading came through a page-summarising tool, which may have missed country sections)
- **How it works:** Eligibility depends on category × country × sometimes state, plus screening and licensing checks per category. "Pre-badge" ads (leads served while verification is still pending, ranked below badged providers) are not available for garage door services, locksmiths or health-care verticals, so these businesses get no LSA leads until verification is complete.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Always check with Google's own eligibility tool at g.co/localservices, entering the business's real category and postcode. Record the result and the date. Do not conclude "not available" from a blog post or an old list, and do not conclude "available" from a category name alone (for example "pet grooming" versus a *mobile* groomer, which is a service-area business).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Some categories exist only in some states. Some country pages carry a different category set. A category being available does not mean every business in it will pass screening.
- **Signals in data:** No LSA unit on the SERP for core queries in the target city (a weak signal: you may simply not have been shown one). Competitors showing an LSA unit with a "Google Verified" badge.
- **Sources:** [Getting started with Local Services Ads – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether "Pet grooming" covers mobile groomers in Texas. Check this in the tool. The full category list for each non-US country was not extracted.

### MKT-LOCAL-004 · "Google Verified" replaced Guaranteed/Screened badges; the money-back guarantee ended; LSA reviews now live in Business Profile
- **Claim:** In October 2025 Google merged its LSA badges (Google Guaranteed, Google Screened, License Verified by Google) into one "Google Verified" badge **(changed 2025-10)**. The Money Back Guarantee ended: consumers can claim reimbursement only for LSA bookings made before 2025-12-07, within 30 days of the service being completed. From 2025-07-11, LSA reviews are managed in Google Business Profile, and existing reviews were re-checked against Maps review policies **(changed 2025-07)**.
- **Evidence type:** documented (badge, end of guarantee); google-statement (review move, from Google's advertiser email as reported)
- **Confidence:** high (badge); medium (review move)
- **How it works:** Existing verified advertisers received the badge automatically. New advertisers get it after screening. The badge shows on the LSA profile and "may dynamically appear on other surfaces". Google's badge page says "Local Services Ads ranking won't be affected by this change". Note that the badge page itself does not name the badges it replaced or give a launch month; the merge list and the October 2025 rollout come from the Search Engine Journal report of Google's announcement. Separately, the Getting Started page says pre-badge listings rank below fully onboarded providers, so completing verification still matters for position.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Stop using "Google Guaranteed" in ad copy, landing pages and client reports. The review count on LSA is now the GBP review count, so GBP review health (MKT-LOCAL-012) directly affects LSA ranking.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Old practitioner guides still describe Guaranteed vs Screened by vertical. They are out of date. The badge is "currently unavailable for businesses in dining verticals" (Food and Beverage, Restaurants, Dessert and Coffee), per the LSA overview page.
- **Signals in data:** Review counts that fell in mid-2025 (reviews removed in the policy re-check).
- **Sources:** [About Google Verified badge – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/16498018?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google confirms new Google Verified badge for Local Services Ads – Search Engine Journal, 2025-08-21](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-confirms-new-google-verified-badge-for-local-services-ads/554360/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google shifts Local Service Ads reviews to Business Profiles management – PPC Land, 2025-07-16](https://ppc.land/google-shifts-local-service-ads-reviews-to-business-profiles-management/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Local Services Ads overview – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7549347?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13; dining exclusion)

### MKT-LOCAL-005 · LSA lead credits are automatic and narrow; there are none in EMEA
- **Claim:** Manual LSA lead disputes were replaced in mid-2024 by automatic credits. Google reassesses charged leads, and they "may be issued credits automatically if later determined to be low quality", usually within 30 days. Advertisers give input through "Rate this lead". Credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" are no longer given. Lead credits are not available for health-care verticals, tax specialists or advertisers in EMEA. Call recordings are also unavailable for all verticals in Europe.
- **Evidence type:** documented; practitioner (2024 transition date)
- **Confidence:** high (current rules); medium (transition date)
- **How it works:** Credits go to the account balance. The original charge still appears on the invoice. One older Help page still says a successfully disputed lead is credited "(US and Canada only)". Read it as legacy wording.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Wrong-service and out-of-area leads are now the advertiser's cost. Keep service categories and service areas tight, because you cannot claim these leads back. For European clients, budget for no credits at all. Rate every lead anyway, since Google says the system adapts to feedback.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Expect less money back than older guides suggest. Agencies offering "LSA dispute recovery" may be selling a process that no longer exists.
- **Signals in data:** Credits line on the invoice. Share of leads that the CRM tags as out of area or wrong service.
- **Sources:** [Lead credits for Local Services Ads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7435486) (checked 2026-10-13); [How charges work – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7195435) (checked 2026-10-13); [Dispute leads – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/11444536) (checked 2026-10-13); [What Google's new automated lead credit system means – Socius, 2024-07-31](https://www.sociusmarketing.com/blog/what-googles-new-automated-lead-credit-system-means-for-your-local-services-ads/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-006 · LSA is moving into Google Ads as a pay-per-lead Performance Max campaign (2026-2027)
- **Claim:** Existing LSA campaigns are being migrated automatically into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign type for pay-per-lead goals **(changed 2026-08)**. The phases are: 2026-08 for selected US home and storefront advertisers; late 2026 for service-area businesses and custom bidding or booking setups; 2027 for non-US accounts and remaining categories. Billing stays per lead. Ads still serve only on Search and Maps with keywordless targeting. The Verified badge carries over.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Manual maximum cost-per-lead bidding is removed. Vertical-level target CPAs become one campaign-level Target CPA, so create separate campaigns where verticals need different targets. Weekly budgets become average daily budgets (weekly ÷ 7) with a monthly cap of daily × 30.4 (the PLT-STR-020 logic). Better Business Bureau callouts are dropped (add at least six structured callouts). Name, address and hours sync from GBP, and a name or address change triggers a 24-48 hour review that can pause the campaign. **Historical performance reports do not migrate**, so export them first. New pay-per-lead campaigns cannot yet be created in Google Ads; Google points to g.co/localservices for now.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Before an account's migration date, export the LSA history. Decide whether one Target CPA can cover the account's services or whether to split campaigns. Freeze GBP name and address edits around migration. Account administrators get notice 14 days before migration and a reminder 7 days later (Search Engine Journal), so the export can be scheduled. A settling-in period after migration is practitioner expectation, not documented.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Mixing LSA-PMax and normal Search campaigns in one account means conversion goals and budgets are now shared tools; a careless account-level goal change can affect both. Google does not document how the two campaign types interact in the auction (MKT-LOCAL-007).
- **Signals in data:** A new PMax campaign appears with pay-per-lead billing. The old LSA dashboard redirects to Google Ads. There is a break in historical comparisons.
- **Sources:** [Local Services Ads transition to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google folds Local Services Ads into Google Ads – PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-folds-local-services-ads-into-google-ads-cuts-historical-reports/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google is bringing Local Services Ads into Google Ads – Search Engine Journal, 2026-07-20](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-is-bringing-local-service-ads-into-google-ads/582816/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether the migrated campaigns share data or learning with other PMax or Search campaigns in the same account.

### MKT-LOCAL-007 · LSA and Search text ads on the same results page: two separate channels, run both only if each pays
- **Claim:** LSA listings show in their own unit at the top of eligible results, separate from text ads. They are priced per lead and ranked by profile quality (MKT-LOCAL-002), while Search ads are priced per click and ranked by Ad Rank. A business can appear in both units. Google's auction documentation says it runs "different auctions for each ad location", but no Google page we opened describes how LSA and Search ads interact or limit each other.
- **Evidence type:** inference (from documented mechanics); documented (separate auctions per ad location)
- **Confidence:** medium
- **How it works:** LSA leads come from the profile (calls, messages and bookings through Google), with no website visit. Search ads send clicks to the site or to a call asset. LSA sits above the text ads, so where it shows, text ads are pushed down the page, which can lower their CTR and raise the bid needed to stay visible. Searches that LSA does not cover still get text ads: off-category jobs, informational and comparison queries, and areas or categories without LSA.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Where LSA is available, test it first or in parallel for urgent, high-intent jobs. It has no website risk and is priced per lead. Use Search ads for jobs LSA does not cover well, for higher-margin specific services, and to land on pages that pre-qualify (prices, service area). Compare the two on cost per booked job, after removing duplicates: the same customer may call through both.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Treating LSA leads and Search leads as additive when they overlap. Judging Search ads on CTR when LSA sits above them. Where LSA is unavailable (for example Israel), the full top of the page belongs to text ads, Maps ads and the local pack.
- **Signals in data:** Search CTR or top impression share falls when LSA starts showing for the same queries. The same caller ID appears in both LSA leads and Search call reports.
- **Sources:** [Google Ads run different auctions for each ad location – Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-srun-different-auctions-for-each-ad-location-452749) (checked 2026-10-13); [How Local Services Ads ranking works – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether Google suppresses one of a business's own ads when its LSA and Search ad are both eligible, and whether that changes after the PMax migration.

### MKT-LOCAL-008 · Local pack ranking: relevance, distance and prominence; it cannot be bought
- **Claim:** Google says local results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and prominence". Relevance is how well the profile matches the search. Distance is how far the business is from the searcher (Google uses "what it knows" about their location if they do not share it). Prominence is how well known the business is, based partly on how many websites link to it and how many reviews it has; "More reviews and positive ratings can help". Google also says: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking."
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high (the factors); low (their weights)
- **How it works:** Distance is fixed by where the business is, and for a service-area business by its hidden address. The things a business can change are relevance (correct primary category, services, complete profile) and prominence (reviews, links, real-world reputation).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Explains why a business ranks well near its base and badly across town, and why ads (Search, Maps) are the main way to reach the outer parts of a service area. Never sell ad spend to a client as a way to improve local-pack ranking.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Practitioner factor surveys add things like keywords in the business name and proximity to the city centre. Keyword stuffing breaks the name rules (MKT-LOCAL-009). Rankings tested from one spot do not show what searchers elsewhere see.
- **Signals in data:** GBP Performance report: searches and calls fall as distance from the base grows (seen with grid-rank tools).
- **Sources:** [Tips to improve your local ranking on Google – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-009 · Business Profile rules: service-area businesses hide their address; the name must be the real-world name
- **Claim:** A service-area business "should hide your business address", for example a plumber working from home. The service area "shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving time" from the base, with exceptions for some businesses. A hybrid may show its address only if it serves customers there during staffed hours. P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes are not allowed. Virtual offices are not eligible unless staffed during business hours. There should be one profile per business. The name must be the real-world name: no taglines, store codes, phone numbers, URLs, location details or all-caps; "unnecessary information" in the name "could result in the suspension" of the profile.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The phone number must reach the specific location and be "under the direct control of the business". Use a local number rather than a central call centre where possible. Premium-rate numbers and redirecting or "referring" URLs are not allowed.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Mobile groomers, locksmiths and mobile mechanics are service-area businesses. Do not show a home address to rank better. Do not rename "Joe's Locksmith" to "Joe's Locksmith 24/7 Emergency Tel Aviv". Both are common reasons for suspension in categories that see a lot of spam (MKT-LOCAL-011).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Legal-entity suffixes and special characters are allowed only with real-world proof (signage, invoices). Businesses selling age-restricted products cannot be service-area-only.
- **Signals in data:** A competitor's keyword-stuffed name outranks the client. You can report it through "Suggest an edit", but repeated self-editing of competitors' listings can get the reporting account flagged (practitioner warning, MKT-LOCAL-011).
- **Sources:** [Guidelines for representing your business on Google – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-010 · Business Profile verification: Google chooses the method; video proof for service-area businesses; some edits trigger re-verification
- **Claim:** Verification methods are phone/SMS, email, live video call, recorded video and postcard. They "are automatically determined by Google and can't be changed", based on business type, public information, region and hours. Recorded video verification must be live from the mobile app, unedited, continuous and at least 30 seconds long. A service-area business must show where it is (street signs or landmarks), that it exists (tools, equipment, branded apparel) and that it is managed by the person verifying (for example "unlocking a van that has your business branding", or a permit or utility bill in the business name). Reviews take up to 5 business days.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** "Changes to your business info may require re-verification." Documented triggers are a name change ("might need to verify again") and a move to a new address ("must verify again"). Editing name, address or category during a pending postcard makes the code invalid. Some businesses must verify with more than one method. In the migrated LSA campaigns, name or address changes also trigger a 24-48 hour review (MKT-LOCAL-006).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Before a launch, check that the client has branded equipment or a vehicle, signage, and documents in the exact business name. A home-based groomer without any of these may fail. Batch and schedule profile edits instead of making many at once.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Live video calls and postcards are not offered to every business. Lists of edits that trigger re-verification beyond name and address are practitioner lore, not documented.
- **Signals in data:** Profile shows "pending" or "verification required" after edits. Calls from the profile drop when it is unverified.
- **Sources:** [Verify your business with a video – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14271705?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Verify your business on Google – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242) (checked 2026-10-13); [Verify your business (postcard) – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7105241?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Edit your Business Profile – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3039617?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-011 · Profile suspensions in high-risk categories (locksmith, garage door): practitioner-observed triggers; one appeal
- **Claim:** Google documents only that profiles that "don't follow our guidelines" may be suspended. The appeals tool states the reason, and an appeal "can only be submitted once", with optional evidence (registration, licence, tax certificate, utility bills whose name and address match the profile). Google publishes no list of high-risk categories for Business Profile. Practitioners report that locksmiths and garage-door businesses are suspended far more often than others. They name as common triggers: many edits to key fields at once, an address with no real presence (PO box, virtual office, co-working), several profiles for one business, a keyword-stuffed name, redirecting website URLs, and policy problems on linked Google accounts.
- **Evidence type:** documented (appeal process); practitioner (triggers, category risk)
- **Confidence:** medium
- **How it works:** These categories attract lead-generation spam with fake locations, so Google's automated checks are stricter. Edits that look like the patterns spammers use trip them.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For locksmith, garage-door, water-damage or towing clients, treat the GBP as fragile. Make one change at a time (practitioners suggest about one per day), prepare documents in the exact name before any appeal, and never create a replacement profile (Google says not to). In Google Ads these same categories may also need advanced verification (MKT-LOCAL-015).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A suspension can come from a competitor's spam report, or from the Google account of a manager. One-shot appeals mean a weak appeal is expensive. Practitioners also list drug rehab and personal-injury law as high-risk.
- **Signals in data:** The profile disappears from Maps. Location assets in Google Ads stop serving. Calls from GBP fall to zero.
- **Sources:** [Fix suspended or disabled profiles – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Top reasons Google suspended your listing – Sterling Sky, 2026-09-23](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/top-reasons-google-my-business-suspended-your-listing/) (checked 2026-10-13). support.google.com/business/answer/9334003 could not be opened on 2026-10-13.

### MKT-LOCAL-012 · Review policy: no incentives, no gating, no staff review drives; fake reviews bring visible restrictions
- **Claim:** Google Maps policy prohibits offering "incentives – such as payment, discounts, free goods and/or services – in exchange for posting any review"; discouraging negative reviews or "selectively solicit[ing] positive reviews" (review gating); pressuring customers to review on the premises; asking staff to collect a set number of reviews; reviews from employees, current or former; and reviews posted on competitors to damage them. Where fake engagement is found, Google may block new reviews for a set period, unpublish existing reviews, and display a warning that fake reviews were removed.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Incentivised or conflicted reviews are removed. Serious or repeated abuse can lead to restrictions on the account or its termination.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Ask *every* customer after the job, using Google's review link, with no reward and no pre-filter ("Happy? Leave a review; unhappy? Call us" is gating). Review tools with a satisfaction step before the Google link carry policy risk. Reviews now feed LSA ranking as well (MKT-LOCAL-004).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Thanking reviewers and replying to reviews is fine. Competitors with obviously bought reviews may look like they win in the short term, but they risk a public warning banner.
- **Signals in data:** Sudden review bursts. Many reviews from accounts with one review each. A consumer warning banner on the profile.
- **Sources:** [Maps user-contributed content policy – Google](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) (checked 2026-10-13); [Business Profile restrictions for policy violations – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/14114287?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-013 · Location assets put Search ads on Google Maps; linking the Business Profile is the route
- **Claim:** Location assets show the address, a map or the distance, and come from a linked Business Profile (or chain or Maps locations). They make Search, Performance Max, Demand Gen and Smart campaigns eligible for Google Maps: square promoted pins, Map search ads and Map suggest ads. You cannot run ads only on Maps. Google's Local ads page says ads can show in search results "when a user clicks to view more local results (for example, by clicking 'More places')". Neither page mentions the local 3-pack itself.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high (eligibility); medium (placement in the local pack, which is not documented)
- **How it works:** Link the Business Profile in Location Manager (Tools > Shared library) or by request to the profile's owner. Locations must pass integrity checks. Profiles marked closed, temporarily or permanently, do not serve. Campaign-level settings override account-level settings. Removing all locations is "not recommended" because it removes location targeting, bidding and store-visit tracking. Maps reporting includes direction, location-detail and call clicks. How Maps clicks are billed (one click type per interaction; any CPM billing for pins under Performance Max store goals) is not stated on the pages opened; treat it as unverified.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For storefronts and hybrids, location assets are low effort and open Maps inventory. For service-area businesses, Google says to set the profile up as a service-area business, and Maps ads have less to show (no pin at a public address). Target and adjust bids near the business, as Google suggests.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A suspended or unverified GBP silently removes location assets. YouTube ads show locations by default unless you opt out.
- **Signals in data:** "Click type" segment showing get-directions and location-detail clicks. Maps impressions with no website sessions.
- **Sources:** [About location assets – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182) (checked 2026-10-13); [Show local search ads on Google Maps – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7040605?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Local ads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3246303?hl=en-GB) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** The current rules for a sponsored listing inside the 3-pack on the main results page. Practitioners report seeing one, but we found no Google page describing it.

### MKT-LOCAL-014 · Call setups: what is new beyond PLT-ADS-019, MEA-CONV-023 and PRC-DIAG-030
- **Claim:** (a) Google forwarding numbers exist in 30 countries, **including Israel and the US**; the Netherlands and Austria are not on the list. (b) In the US and Canada, "Calls from ads" are now judged by **AI-qualified call leads**. Call recording is on by default (except where it was turned off, and in health-care or financial-services accounts), AI judges intent from the recording, and the minimum call length is now only a fallback for calls that cannot be recorded **(changed 2026-04)**. (c) On desktop a call asset shows a "Call us" button that reveals the number and a QR code. Taps on the call button cost the same as headline clicks. (d) A call asset may show as a call-ad-style button when the campaign uses RSAs, optimises for call conversions or uses Smart Bidding. (e) Call reporting records the caller's number for calls over 15 seconds, except in India (caller ID also fails on some calls in Japan). Calling a forwarding number by dialling it manually is not charged.
- **Evidence type:** documented; press (launch date)
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** "Calls from ads" (calls from the ad itself, via call assets, location assets or call ads) and website calls to a forwarding number after an ad click are separate conversion actions (MEA-CONV-023). Call assets can be scheduled to set days and hours ("only when your business can accept calls"). Recording works only when both numbers are in the US or Canada. Elsewhere, including Israel, duration is still the rule.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** For US clients, check that recording is legal and acceptable for the business (caller notice, sensitive services), then review the AI tags against actual bookings before trusting them for bidding. Expect a break in call-conversion history when the mode changes. Outside the US and Canada, set the duration from real call data (MEA-CONV-023). Schedule call assets to staffed hours (PRC-DIAG-029). Call-only ads cannot be created since 2026-02 and stop serving in 2027-02 (PRC-DIAG-030).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Call reporting (forwarding numbers) is "only available on the Search Network", and call assets added to Display campaigns "may not serve". If forwarding is not available in the country, Google says to disable call reporting to use call assets.
- **Signals in data:** Call conversions change level in April-May 2026 with stable call volume. "#HighIntent" style tags in call details. Desktop "Call us" clicks with no call.
- **Sources:** [Google forwarding number – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2382961?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About AI-qualified call leads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16913326) (checked 2026-10-13); [Measure calls from ads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6095882?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About call assets – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453991?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About call reporting – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454052?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About call ads – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6341403?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google Ads now uses AI to qualify phone call leads – PPC Land](https://ppc.land/google-ads-now-uses-ai-to-qualify-phone-call-leads/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Default minimum call length: none of the Help pages opened states one. The common "60 seconds" default is unverified.

### MKT-LOCAL-015 · Ad policy for local services: advanced verification in the US/Canada; locksmith ads banned in five European countries
- **Claim:** Google's "Other restricted businesses: Local services" policy lists: **United States**: locksmith services and garage door repair require advanced verification. **Canada**: locksmith services require advanced verification. **Spain, Germany, Sweden, Belgium**: locksmith ads "are not able to run". **Netherlands**: locksmith and plumber ads "are not able to run". Elsewhere, local-service ads "are able to run" without this verification. HVAC and pest control are not listed, and Israel is not mentioned.
- **Evidence type:** documented (policy); practitioner (process and timing)
- **Confidence:** high (as of the check date); policy lists change
- **How it works:** You apply through a Google Ads form, entering the Ads account ID and, where one exists, the LSA account. An agency help page describes the steps as: a state business registration in the legal name (plus a DBA filing if the trading name differs), possible follow-up requests (for example proof of address), one appeal if denied, and re-verification about every 12 months or after major changes. It reports timing of "2-4 weeks" up to "2-3 months". Historically (2017 Beta), verification was applied at metro level: in June 2017 Google *removed* verification for plumbers in San Diego and Los Angeles and *added* it for locksmiths, towing, garage door and auto glass repair in the San Francisco Bay Area.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** A US locksmith or garage-door client cannot launch Search ads until verified. Put that delay into the plan, and use GBP and LSA (if verified there) in the meantime. A Tel Aviv locksmith faces no ad-policy verification under this page, but its GBP is still high-risk (MKT-LOCAL-011). Never target a banned country with a locksmith ad through a generic "home services" wording; that is circumvention.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Other policies still apply: misrepresentation of pricing (MKT-NICHE-019); third-party consumer tech support and call-forwarding or recording services, which are not allowed; bail bonds, which are not allowed.
- **Signals in data:** Ads disapproved or "Eligible (limited)" with a local-services restriction. Zero impressions in the restricted country.
- **Sources:** [Other restricted businesses: Local services – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527633) (checked 2026-10-13); [Other restricted businesses – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6368711?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Local services verification Beta update, 2017 – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7382265?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Google Ads advanced verification for locksmith and garage door services – Marketing 360 Help](https://help.marketing360.com/portal/en/kb/articles/googleadsadvancedverificationforlocksmithservicesandgaragedoorservices6865776602954) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-016 · Lead marketplaces and directories: competitors for the same searcher and possible lead sources; compare them on cost per booked job
- **Claim:** Marketplaces and directories both compete with the client in search results and sell leads to it. The models differ. Thumbtack says pros "pay to send a quote", and customers get quotes "from up to 5 pros". A contractor-software vendor (2026) adds that leads are charged on match or contact, are shared, carry no refund if no job follows, and run about $10 to over $100 by pros' reports, since Thumbtack does not publish prices. Angi leads are described by a vendor as shared with about 3-4 contractors, charged per lead, with limited disputes; we could not open any Angi source for this. Yelp's ad pricing page could not be opened. In Israel, Midrag is a review-led directory (about 3.1M recommendations on about 12,400 professionals, with plumbers, AC and electricians the largest sectors). Easy (easy.co.il) is a local search engine with paid promotion. b144 could not be opened.
- **Evidence type:** documented (marketplace's own pages, where opened); practitioner/vendor (pricing, sharing); inference (comparison method)
- **Confidence:** low-medium (prices and rules not verified at the source for Angi, Yelp, b144 or the Israeli platforms)
- **How it works:** Compare every channel on **cost per booked job = cost per lead ÷ (contact rate × booking rate)**, adding fixed fees spread over the jobs. Shared leads have lower booking rates because the customer phones several pros. Exclusive channels (LSA, own Search ads, GBP calls) usually book better, but cost differs. Illustrative only: a $35 shared lead at 60% contact × 25% booking = $233 per job. A $70 LSA lead at 90% × 50% = $156. A $6 CPC at 10% CVR × 80% contact × 50% booking = $150. The real inputs must come from the client's CRM.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** In research, check which marketplaces rank organically and advertise on the core queries. A dense marketplace presence raises competition for clicks and sets customers' expectation of getting several quotes. If the client already buys marketplace leads, their booking rate there is the best pre-launch prior for value (MKT-NICHE-026). In Israel, review platforms such as Midrag affect trust. A good rating there is a landing-page proof point, not a lead channel to model like LSA.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Comparing cost per lead across shared and exclusive channels. Ignoring the time sales staff spend chasing shared leads. Assuming marketplace prices or rules from blog posts are current.
- **Signals in data:** Marketplace domains in Auction insights or in the top organic results. Client's CRM booking rate by lead source.
- **Sources:** [Thumbtack for pros – Thumbtack](https://www.thumbtack.com/pro) (checked 2026-10-13); [What is Thumbtack and how does it work – Housecall Pro, 2026-05-05](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/what-is-thumbtack-how-it-works/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Angi leads for contractors – NextPhone, 2026-07-29](https://www.getnextphone.com/blog/angi-leads-for-contractors) (checked 2026-10-13; vendor, uncited); [Midrag](https://www.midrag.co.il/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Easy](https://www.easy.co.il/) (checked 2026-10-13). Not opened: help.thumbtack.com (empty render), Yelp support (robots-blocked), b144.co.il (robots-blocked).
- **Open questions:** Current pricing models of Angi, Yelp, Midrag, b144 and Easy, from their own pages.

### MKT-LOCAL-017 · Phone handling decides local results: answer rate, speed to lead, staffed-hours scheduling (practitioner)
- **Claim:** For small local service businesses, the share of calls answered live and the speed of the callback often move cost per booked job more than any bid change. Practitioners commonly aim to answer most business-hours calls live (targets around 80-95% are typical in agency talk; no study opened supports a specific threshold), to call back missed calls within minutes, and to switch call-heavy ads off when nobody can answer.
- **Evidence type:** practitioner; study (vendor data)
- **Confidence:** medium (direction); low (thresholds)
- **How it works:** A missed call usually goes to the next competitor. Since 2026-10 a missed LSA call can also be charged (MKT-LOCAL-001), and missed calls lower LSA ranking (MKT-LOCAL-002). Vendor data: Quo's analysis of 16.7M missed calls on its platform (2026) found that only 31% were returned within 48 hours. A CallRail survey of 1,000 US consumers (2025) is cited second-hand with different figures by different vendors: Beside reports that 42% of unanswered callers leave a voicemail and 21% immediately call another business; Quo cites the same survey for 82% "would call a competitor" and 78% "have abandoned a business" after an unanswered call. We did not open the CallRail source. Beside notes that no reliable industry-wide missed-call rate is published. The widely cited "62% of calls go unanswered" has no traceable source that we opened. See MKT-WEB-025 for speed-to-lead evidence and its limits.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Before launch, mystery-call the business at different hours and count rings and outcomes. Match the GBP hours, the LSA schedule and the call-asset schedule to real staffing (PRC-DIAG-029, PLT-STR-013). Route overflow to an answering service or text-back. Use the duration or AI-qualified call conversion (MKT-LOCAL-014), not raw calls. For a one-person mobile business (a groomer mid-appointment), prefer message or form leads with a fast reply, and schedule calls around appointments.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Answering services that cannot book or quote can lower the booking rate. Turning ads off after hours loses emergency jobs that an on-call locksmith could take. For true 24/7 emergency trades, run night hours on a separate campaign with its own target. Vendor statistics come from companies selling answering tools.
- **Signals in data:** Call details with short durations or "not connected". LSA responsiveness warnings. Booked jobs well below calls. Conversions clustered in hours when the owner is on jobs.
- **Sources:** [Small business callback statistics – Quo, 2026-08-20](https://www.quo.com/blog/small-business-callback-statistics/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Missed calls cost small business – Beside, 2026-07-09](https://www.beside.com/blog/missed-calls-cost-small-business) (checked 2026-10-13); [How charges work – Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/7195435) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-018 · Service radius should follow drive time and job value, not a round number of kilometres (practitioner)
- **Claim:** For mobile and call-out services, the profitable area is set by travel time between jobs and by job value, not by a straight-line radius. Practitioners often start with the area reachable in about 20-40 minutes for low-ticket recurring work (grooming, cleaning) and wider for high-ticket or emergency work (locksmith, garage door), then tier bids or campaigns by zone. Google's own GBP limit (about 2 hours of driving) is a listing rule, not an advertising recommendation.
- **Evidence type:** practitioner; inference; documented (GBP 2-hour rule)
- **Confidence:** medium (mechanism); low (ranges)
- **How it works:** Each extra minute of driving uses capacity that could be another appointment. For a mobile groomer doing 4-6 appointments a day, an extra 30 minutes of driving per job can remove one paid slot. Traffic makes a fixed radius very uneven in drive time (Tel Aviv rush hour, Austin I-35). Google's location targeting also includes people "interested in" the area by default (PLT-STR-006), which brings out-of-area leads.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Ask the client for the zones they will actually serve and any travel surcharge. Target by postcode or drive-time-shaped areas. Use "Presence" targeting. Tier bids or campaigns by zone (PRC-DIAG-032). Keep the GBP service area, the LSA area and the ad targeting the same, since LSA no longer credits out-of-area leads (MKT-LOCAL-005).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Too tight an area starves the campaign of volume. Emergency trades can afford long drives on high-ticket jobs. Customers in a border zone may be profitable only for some services.
- **Signals in data:** Matched-locations and distance reports. CRM job addresses outside the zone. Lower booking rate or longer jobs in outer zones.
- **Sources:** [Guidelines for representing your business on Google – Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) (checked 2026-10-13) (2-hour rule); practitioner ranges, no study opened.

### MKT-LOCAL-019 · Pre-badge LSA: most categories can get leads before verification finishes
- **Claim:** While screening and licence checks are still pending, LSA can serve "pre-badge" ads after preliminary checks. These are ranked below providers that already carry the Google Verified badge. Pre-badge is not available for garage door services, locksmiths or health-care categories, which get no LSA leads until verification is complete.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** medium-high (the rule is on Google's page; how soon pre-badge ads start and how many leads they get is not published)
- **How it works:** For an eligible category (plumbers, HVAC, electricians and most home services), applying on day 1 can produce leads within the first weeks, at lower positions. For excluded categories, the full verification time is the wait.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** In a month-1 plan, budget LSA from the week it is likely to serve, not from the end of verification, so the budget table must still sum to the cap if LSA runs from week 1. For excluded categories (locksmiths, garage doors), plan Search or other channels to cover the verification wait. Do not tell a client "LSA may not start this month" without checking which case applies.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Preliminary checks can still fail. Pre-badge positions may produce few leads in a crowded metro.
- **Signals in data:** Leads arriving before the badge shows in the LSA dashboard; lower lead volume than badged competitors at similar budgets.
- **Sources:** [Getting started with Local Services Ads – Local Services Help](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### MKT-LOCAL-020 · Lead and call resellers (pay-per-call, referral): policy risk comes before economics
- **Claim:** Three Google Ads policies bear on a business that sells calls or leads to local providers instead of doing the work. (1) "Call directory, forwarding, and recording services": "Promotions for call directory, forwarding, and recording services are not allowed"; the page's example is calling a number to be connected to another service. Allowed exceptions: online directories of phone numbers, call recording software, subscriptions for businesses, and businesses advertising their own official numbers. The page does not mention pay-per-call lead generation for local services, so whether it covers it is an interpretation we could not settle. (2) Misrepresentation: the ad and page must not hide who the advertiser is or present the providers' reviews, prices or licences as the advertiser's own. (3) Advanced verification (MKT-LOCAL-015): locksmiths in the US and Canada, garage door repair in the US only.
- **Evidence type:** documented (policy pages); inference (how they apply to resellers)
- **Confidence:** high (wording, as of the check date); low (how Google enforces (1) against local-service call resellers)
- **How it works:** Policy decides whether the account can run at all, so it is checked before any economics. The safer setup states plainly on the page and in a call greeting that the advertiser connects callers with independent local companies, uses its own brand, and shows only its own claims. A long clean history running the same model is evidence that Google has tolerated it, not that it is allowed.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Any plan for a pay-per-call, referral or lead-generation advertiser names the suspension risk in its verdict, links the policy pages, and keeps the launch reversible (one campaign, its own account if the main account carries other business). For locksmith work in the US or Canada, plan for the verification delay.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Treating "nobody got suspended yet" as permission. Copying a provider's reviews or "licensed and insured" claims. Dynamic number swapping on the page can also trigger disapprovals of call assets (PLT-ADS-019).
- **Signals in data:** Disapprovals or suspensions citing misrepresentation, "unacceptable business practices" or call forwarding; a verification request.
- **Sources:** [Call directory, forwarding, and recording services – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527435) (checked 2026-10-15); [Other restricted businesses: Local services – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/13527633) (checked 2026-10-15); [Advanced verification – Google Advertising Policies Help](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/7167922) (checked 2026-10-15)
- **Open questions:** How Google enforces the call-forwarding policy against local-service pay-per-call advertisers; whether any official page addresses that model directly.

## Common mistakes and misleading metrics
- Comparing LSA, marketplaces and Search on cost per lead instead of cost per booked job, or comparing shared leads with exclusive ones.
- Setting GBP hours to "24 hours" without night cover, now that LSA charges missed calls of more than 20 seconds during business hours.
- Using "Google Guaranteed" in copy or reports after the 2025 switch to "Google Verified", or promising clients LSA dispute refunds that are no longer available.
- Assuming LSA exists in a country (for example Israel) or for a category without checking g.co/localservices. Assuming it does not exist because one list omitted it.
- Keyword-stuffing the GBP name or showing a home address for a service-area business. Both are common reasons for suspension in high-risk trades.
- Gated or incentivised review requests.
- Launching US locksmith or garage-door Search ads before advanced verification, or advertising locksmiths in Spain, Germany, Sweden, Belgium or the Netherlands.
- Reading "Calls from ads" trends across April 2026 in the US/Canada without noting the switch to AI-qualified calls.
- Selling ad spend as a way to improve local-pack ranking.
