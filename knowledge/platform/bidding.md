# Bidding strategies, learning, conversion delay and scaling

This file covers how each Google Ads bid strategy works, what Smart Bidding learns from, how learning and conversion delay distort recent results, how to set and change targets, and how to scale. The most important idea: Smart Bidding works toward an *average* target, or toward spending the whole budget, by setting a bid for each auction. Since 17 August 2026, a Target CPA or Target ROAS campaign that is limited by budget no longer beats its target by default. Read recent CPA or ROAS only after the conversion delay has passed, and compare it with the *average target* the bidder actually used, not the target you typed in.

### PLT-BID-001 · Strategy landscape and the June 2026 renaming
- **Claim:** Google groups strategies by goal. The **clicks/visibility** strategies are Maximize clicks and Target impression share. The **conversion-based Smart Bidding** strategies are Maximize conversions, Target CPA, Maximize conversion value and Target ROAS. Manual CPC is the only remaining manual option for Search. Target CPC exists only in Demand Gen. Pay-per-conversion is Display-only. **(changed 2026-06)** Google is relabelling "Maximize conversions with a Target CPA" as "Target CPA" and "Maximize conversion value with a Target ROAS" as "Target ROAS". Only the UI label changes; bidding behaviour does not. The API, Editor and mobile app lag behind the UI.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** In the current UI, Target CPA is Maximize conversions with an optional CPA target, and Target ROAS is Maximize conversion value with an optional ROAS target. Without a target, the strategy tries to spend the full budget. With a target, it aims for an average efficiency.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Choose the strategy family by objective (visits, visibility, conversion count, conversion value) and by whether you want a spend constraint (budget) or an efficiency constraint (target). Expect older documents, scripts and API fields to use either name.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** During the transition, UI names and API enum names (e.g. MAXIMIZE_CONVERSIONS with target_cpa_micros) will not match. Tools that parse strategy names need both.
- **Signals in data:** Bid strategy type column; in the API, a `maximize_conversions.target_cpa_micros` value > 0 means it is effectively Target CPA.
- **Sources:** [Choose your bid strategy](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2979071) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategies to help you meet your goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10969287?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC Land: Google Ads gives advertisers 6 weeks before CPA targets double](https://ppc.land/google-ads-gives-advertisers-6-weeks-before-cpa-targets-double/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-002 · Manual CPC and the end of Enhanced CPC
- **Claim:** **(changed 2025-03)** Enhanced CPC (ECPC) was removed for Search and Display campaigns in the week of 31 March 2025. Campaigns that were not migrated "are now effectively using Manual CPC". Shopping lost ECPC earlier (2023, per SEL). Manual CPC has no learning period and uses your own bids and bid adjustments.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** ECPC raised or lowered manual bids based on predicted conversion likelihood. Without it, Manual CPC bids are static per keyword or ad group, apart from your bid adjustments. Google's suggested replacements are Maximize conversions/Target CPA, Maximize conversion value/Target ROAS, Maximize clicks/Target CPM, or pay-per-conversion (Display).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Advice to "use ECPC to build data before Smart Bidding" is stale for Search; even an Optmyzr guide from July 2026 still repeats it. Manual CPC still has valid uses: very low conversion volume, broken or untrustworthy conversion tracking, strict CPC control on brand, or tests that need fixed bids. Any campaign still showing ECPC history from before April 2025 is now running Manual CPC.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The deprecation note covers Search and Display. Hotel and other legacy bidding types were not checked.
- **Signals in data:** A campaign that used ECPC until March 2025 and was never migrated. Its bids stopped reacting to conversion signals, so CPA and CPC may have shifted from April 2025.
- **Sources:** [Enhanced CPC (ECPC): Definition](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2390590?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: Google Ads to deprecate enhanced CPC for Search and Display Ads](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-deprecate-enhanced-cpc-search-display-446350) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-003 · What Smart Bidding optimises and which signals it uses
- **Claim:** Smart Bidding sets a separate bid for each auction ("auction-time bidding"). The bid comes from a predicted conversion rate (and, for value strategies, predicted value). That prediction uses signals such as device, physical location, location intent, weekday and time, remarketing list membership and recency, ad characteristics, interface language, browser, OS, and the actual search query (Search/Shopping). Some signals apply only to certain campaign types: Search partner, Display placement and site behaviour, product attributes, price competitiveness, and seasonality (Shopping).
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The models are trained across your account. Google says "Smart Bidding can optimize based on data from all of your campaigns", so new campaigns benefit from existing data. With little conversion data, it starts from query-level conversion-rate models and refines them with Bayesian learning as more granular data arrives. Smart Bidding learns from every conversion action in the "Conversions" column (actions with "Include in Conversions" set), even when they belong to different goals.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Which actions count as primary conversions is the main lever on what the bidder chases. A cheap micro-conversion set as primary will soak up bids. Manual bid adjustments for location, audience or schedule are mostly ignored. The exceptions are device adjustments, which change the target (PLT-BID-024), and -100% device exclusions.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Bidder signals are not visible per auction. The bid strategy report's "Top signals" shows only some dimensions and is often missing. Google lists Search-partner and placement signals, but placement-level bid control is not available.
- **Signals in data:** Bid strategy report > Top signals (device, location, day, time). A primary action with a sudden volume jump usually leads to a CPA drop on paper and a shift in spend.
- **Sources:** [About Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) (checked 2026-10-13); [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategy report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7074566) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-004 · Maximize conversions / Maximize conversion value: budget-spending by design
- **Claim:** Without a target, Maximize conversions (or value) "will try to fully spend your average daily budget". Google calls these strategies limited by budget *by design*, so it does not recommend the "Search lost IS (budget)" column for them. Switching a campaign that underspends its budget to these strategies can raise spend sharply.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The budget is the constraint. The bidder buys the conversions it predicts are cheapest per unit first, then adds pricier ones until the budget is used. CPA or ROAS is therefore an *output* that changes with budget size: more budget pushes the bidder further down the marginal-return curve.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use these strategies when spend is fixed and efficiency can float. They also suit new campaigns with no target history, and campaigns used to gather data before setting a target. Practitioners treat budget as the efficiency dial here: cut budget to improve average CPA, raise it to buy volume at a higher marginal cost. After the August 2026 change, Google recommends them for an inflexible budget when you can accept ROI changing with budget (PLT-BID-007).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Very large budgets relative to demand produce high CPCs and poor marginal CPA. There is no target to stop the spend. With a shared budget, all campaigns must use the same portfolio strategy, or the status shows Misconfigured (PLT-BID-020).
- **Signals in data:** Spend close to 100% of the daily budget every day, CPC rising after a budget increase, CPA moving with budget changes.
- **Sources:** [About Maximize conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7381968) (checked 2026-10-13); [Changes to target based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17061251?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-005 · Target CPA: an average, not a cap
- **Claim:** Target CPA aims for as many conversions as possible at an **average** cost per conversion equal to the target. Individual conversions can cost more or less. On a given day it may spend up to 2x the average daily budget, but no more than 30.4x the daily budget over a month. Google recommends starting from the last 30 days' average CPA, adjusted for conversion delay. It warns that a target set too low loses clicks and conversions.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Each auction gets a bid that reflects predicted conversion rate × target, plus the bidder's pacing toward the average. If the campaign has been running below target, bids can rise to win more conversions. Report against "Avg. target CPA", the traffic-weighted target actually used, which includes device adjustments, ad-group targets and changes made during the period. Do not report against the number you typed in.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use it when conversions are roughly equal in value and you have a CPA ceiling. Google advises against ad-group targets because they restrict Smart Bidding. Demand Gen is the exception: there, ad-group targets override the campaign target.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A target far below what the market supports gives low impression volume, not cheap conversions. Volume falls instead of CPA improving. Google says it can run with no conversion history, but performance is then driven by modelled rates (PLT-BID-016).
- **Signals in data:** Actual CPA vs Avg. target CPA in the bid strategy report. A big gap on low volume may not be statistically significant; the report itself suggests a longer date range.
- **Sources:** [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategy report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7074566) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-006 · Target ROAS / Maximize conversion value: predicted value per auction
- **Claim:** Target ROAS predicts the value of each potential conversion at auction time. It sets bids to maximise conversion value while averaging the target ROAS (conversion value ÷ spend × 100%; $5 revenue per $1 spend = 500%). The documented minimum data before switching differs by campaign type. For example: 15 conversions in 30 days for Search and Shopping; 50 in 35 days per campaign (10 of them in the last 7 days) for Demand Gen; 10 per day or 300 in 30 days for App.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The bidder multiplies predicted conversion probability by predicted value. Auctions with high expected value get higher bids. The target ROAS sets how much of that value it will pay. Google says to report conversion values for 4 weeks or 1–2 conversion cycles, whichever is longer, before moving from Target CPA. The target should be "at or below" historical ROAS, measured without the most recent conversion-delay period.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use it when conversion values really differ (basket size, lead quality, margin). If all values are equal, Target CPA does the same job with fewer failure points (Optmyzr). Google's internal 2021 data: advertisers who moved from Target CPA to Target ROAS saw about 14% more conversion value at similar ROAS. This is Google-reported and not independently verified.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Value stacking, where several primary actions each carry a value for one sale, inflates ROAS. Values that do not reflect profit make the bidder chase revenue, not margin. Very spiky values (a few huge orders) make prediction noisy.
- **Signals in data:** Avg. target ROAS vs actual ROAS. A ROAS jump right after a value-rule or value-setting change is a reporting artefact, not a real improvement.
- **Sources:** [About Target ROAS bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) (checked 2026-10-13); [Tips on measuring Smart Bidding performance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268633) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategies to help you meet your goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10969287?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-007 · Budget-limited Target CPA/ROAS now converge to the target
- **Claim:** **(changed 2026-08)** Google changed how Target CPA and Target ROAS behave when the campaign is limited by budget. Before, such campaigns often **beat** their target (lower CPA or higher ROAS than set), and that over-performance moved unpredictably when budgets changed. From 17 August 2026 (global by 27 August), they optimise "more consistently toward the target you entered". A campaign beating its target will drift toward it. Google's example: a $10 target CPA running at $5 moves toward $10. Google did **not** change any targets or budgets automatically.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Previously, a budget cap made the bidder bid more cautiously, so it bought only the cheapest conversions and effectively used a tighter target than the one set. Now the bidder bids to the stated target. The budget still caps total spend, but the efficiency of that spend moves to the target. Affected: Search, Shopping, Performance Max, Demand Gen and Travel; Target CPC in Demand Gen. Display and Hotel already worked this way. Not affected: App, Video reach/view, Manual CPC, Target IS, Target CPM, and campaigns not limited by budget. The auction itself did not change. In Performance Max and Demand Gen, spend may shift across channels.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Google's options for each budget-limited campaign: keep the target, lower it to recent actuals (e.g. $5), set a custom target (e.g. $7), or switch to Maximize conversions or Maximize conversion value. For portfolios and shared budgets, adjust at the portfolio or shared-budget level. Google says aligning a target with past performance should not cause noticeable volatility, so it does not need to be phased in. Google advises against adding data exclusions or bid limits just in response to this change. Campaigns with fewer than 7 conversions get no recommended target and need a manual review.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Industry reaction was split. Some practitioners called it a "money grab" (PPC Land, unnamed). Accounts with stale targets set years ago will see CPA rise or ROAS fall. Google's "Bid Target Adjustment Tool" and its notifications started 6 July 2026; the main help page names no tool, but PPC Land and ppcnewsfeed describe it.
- **Signals in data:** Budget-limited target campaigns whose CPA rose, or ROAS fell, toward the set target after 17–27 Aug 2026 with no other change. Compare before 17 Aug with after 27 Aug, after allowing 1–2 conversion cycles.
- **Sources:** [Changes to target based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17061251?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC Land: 6 weeks before CPA targets double](https://ppc.land/google-ads-gives-advertisers-6-weeks-before-cpa-targets-double/) (checked 2026-10-13); [Digital Applied: Google Ads changed bidding on budget-limited campaigns](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/google-ads-budget-limited-campaigns-bid-change-what-to-check) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** How large the average drift was in practice. No independent dataset had been published by 2026-10-13. Also unclear: whether "limited by budget" is judged per day or over a longer period.

### PLT-BID-008 · Target vs budget: which one is the control lever
- **Claim:** For Target CPA and Target ROAS, the **target** sets efficiency and the budget mainly caps volume. Google: "Adjusting bids and budgets is the best way to control performance". After the August 2026 change, Google recommends "demand-led budgeting": daily budgets set comfortably above average spend, which need not spend in full. For Maximize strategies without a target, the **budget** is the efficiency lever.
- **Evidence type:** documented (budget headroom, demand-led budgeting); inference (lever framing)
- **Confidence:** medium-high
- **How it works:** With budget headroom, a target strategy spends only on auctions whose predicted value supports the target. Raising the budget does nothing if the campaign is already target-constrained. Lowering a target CPA, or raising a target ROAS, cuts volume. Loosening the target buys more volume at a higher marginal cost.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Decision rule (inference): if a target campaign is limited by budget and you are happy with the target, add budget. If it is not limited by budget and you want more volume, loosen the target. If the budget is fixed and you want the best efficiency, use a Maximize strategy and accept that efficiency moves with budget. Avoid running target strategies permanently budget-capped. Since August 2026 that no longer brings hidden over-performance, only lost volume at the target.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Strict monthly caps such as client invoices or fixed grants can make "budget headroom" impossible. In that case, use a Maximize strategy or tighter targets. Shared budgets complicate this further.
- **Signals in data:** Status "Limited (budget constrained)" on a target strategy. Daily spend far below budget means the campaign is target-constrained, so raising the budget will not add volume.
- **Sources:** [About Target ROAS bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-009 · Maximize clicks: traffic objective and its quality pitfalls
- **Claim:** Maximize clicks adjusts max CPCs to get as many clicks as possible within budget. It applies one bid across the strategy's keywords and moves it up or down to spend the budget. It does **not** optimise for conversions or impression share. An optional max CPC bid limit caps the bids, but a limit set too low can stop the strategy from reaching its goal.
- **Evidence type:** documented (mechanics); inference (quality pitfalls)
- **Confidence:** high (mechanics); medium (pitfalls)
- **How it works:** Every click has the same value to the objective, so the cheapest clicks are the most attractive. The likely result (inference) is more spend on low-CPC queries, broad-match variants, Search partners (if enabled) and lower positions, where clicks are cheap but intent can be weaker. The strategy also does not tell mobile clicks from desktop clicks. Google notes that if click share is already very high, it may add little traffic.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Practitioners use it for a short time to collect traffic and query data for a new campaign without conversion tracking. They also use it for pure traffic goals, and they set a max CPC cap so a few expensive auctions do not eat the budget. Before switching to Smart Bidding once conversions arrive, review search terms and Search-partner traffic.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** A cap set far below the going CPC gives almost no impressions. Without a cap, CPCs on competitive terms can spike to spend the budget. Not available as a portfolio, and without bid limits, in Demand Gen.
- **Signals in data:** Low CTR, high bounce rate and low conversion rate together with rising click volume. A larger share of clicks from Search partners or long-tail queries. Spend pinned to budget with a low average CPC.
- **Sources:** [About Maximize clicks bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268626?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategies to help you meet your goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10969287?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** No study was opened that measures the conversion-rate or traffic-quality gap between Maximize clicks and Smart Bidding on the same campaigns.

### PLT-BID-010 · Target impression share: visibility at a price
- **Claim:** Target impression share sets bids to show the ad in a chosen placement (absolute top, top, or anywhere on the page) for a target percentage of eligible auctions. For example, 65% at the absolute top, or 100% on brand. It ignores bid adjustments except -100% device. It counts only the Google Search Network (no partners). A max CPC bid limit is optional, but Google warns not to set it too low.
- **Evidence type:** documented (mechanics); practitioner/inference (pitfalls)
- **Confidence:** high (mechanics); medium (pitfalls)
- **How it works:** The bidder raises bids until it wins the target share. It does not consider whether the auction converts. If a competitor bids aggressively for the same slot, holding a high absolute-top share can push CPCs up with no ceiling other than the max CPC limit. In August 2026, Target IS was explicitly excluded from the budget-limited target change.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use it for brand-defence and visibility goals, always with a max CPC cap. Practitioners avoid it for non-brand conversion goals because impression share is not the business outcome. On brand, compare its CPC with what Manual CPC or Target CPA achieves.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Low-volume or highly competitive terms can produce extreme CPCs. A 100% target is often unreachable (e.g. because of ad rank thresholds or low Quality Score), and the bidder then keeps pushing up to the cap. Impression share includes only eligible auctions, so restrictive targeting can make the share look high while reach stays small.
- **Signals in data:** CPC creeping up while absolute-top IS stays flat. Status "Limited (bid limits)". Auction insights show a competitor whose position-above rate has risen.
- **Sources:** [About Target impression share bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9121108?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-011 · "Learning" status: what triggers it, and what does not
- **Claim:** The status shows "Learning" for four documented reasons: **new strategy** (created or reactivated), **setting change**, **composition change** (campaigns, ad groups or keywords added or removed), and **ad-group target change** (Shopping). It does not apply to Manual CPC. Google also says that changing a CPA or ROAS target "won't trigger a 'learning' status" and "won't reset anything Smart Bidding has already learned".
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high (triggers); medium (exact meaning of "setting change", since the two documents are not reconciled)
- **How it works:** Learning is the period in which the strategy recalibrates to a new goal. Google stresses that the algorithms keep learning after the status clears. The status is a label about expected volatility, not a switch between two modes.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Do not judge results while the status is "Learning". Group structural changes together so the bidder recalibrates once instead of several times. Target tweaks are not "resets" according to Google. The common belief that "any edit resets learning" is folklore.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Changing conversion goals or primary actions changes what the bidder optimises for. Google's guidance on conversion-goal changes now covers Search, Shopping and Performance Max, so expect recalibration even if the status label does not change. Not shown for Hotel campaigns.
- **Signals in data:** The bid strategy status column (hover for the reason). CPA or ROAS volatility around the date of a change.
- **Sources:** [Bid strategy statuses](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6263057) (checked 2026-10-13); [Duration of the learning period](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501) (checked 2026-10-13); [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: Google Ads reframes Smart Bidding learning around 50 conversions](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-reframes-smart-bidding-learning-around-50-conversions-490382) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-012 · Learning duration: documented in conversion cycles, now also around 50 conversions
- **Claim:** **(changed 2026-09)** How long learning lasts depends on conversion volume, conversion-cycle length and the strategy type. Google's guidance is in flux. The en-US help page still says "a few conversion cycles (1-2 typically)". The en-GB version of the same page, and industry coverage from 22 Sept 2026, say it "can take up to around 50 conversion events or 3 conversion cycles". Earlier versions were described as "up to 3 weeks or 1–2 cycles". Learning can be faster with more conversion data.
- **Evidence type:** documented (both versions opened); practitioner (folklore)
- **Confidence:** medium (the text differs between localised versions)
- **How it works:** A conversion cycle is the time from click to conversion. The bidder needs enough completed cycles, or enough conversion events, to measure its new operating point. A low-volume account with a 30-day cycle can therefore "learn" for months. A high-volume e-commerce account with same-day conversions may settle within days.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Set the evaluation window to the *larger* of about 50 conversions or 2–3 conversion cycles, not a fixed "7 days" or "2 weeks". Those fixed windows are folklore, partly carried over from Meta's 50-events-in-7-days rule. For low-volume accounts, accept a longer calendar time and avoid stacking changes. SEL reads the 50 as an approximate upper benchmark, not a requirement.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The status may clear before performance is stable, or results may stabilise while the status still shows. Performance Max can take longer when most traffic is outside Search and Shopping.
- **Signals in data:** Days-to-conversion distribution (PLT-BID-014). Conversions accumulated since the change.
- **Sources:** [Duration of the learning period (en)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Duration of the learning period (en-GB)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13020501?hl=en-GB) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: reframes learning around 50 conversions](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-reframes-smart-bidding-learning-around-50-conversions-490382) (checked 2026-10-13); [ppcnewsfeed: Google updates bid strategy learning period](https://ppcnewsfeed.com/ppc-news/2026-09/google-updates-bid-strategy-learning-period/) (checked 2026-10-13); [TechWyse: 50-conversion calibration benchmark](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/google-ads-smart-bidding-learning-period-50-conversions) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether "50 conversion events" counts conversions in the campaign, in the portfolio or across the account, and whether it counts primary actions only.

### PLT-BID-013 · How Smart Bidding handles conversion delay
- **Claim:** Smart Bidding uses "adaptive historical weighting". It relies more on recent data, but gives less weight to recent clicks that have not yet had time to convert, which "helps prevent overreactions to recent clicks that are experiencing conversion delays". Accounts with long conversion cycles (car dealers, travel) get less weight on recent data than accounts with short cycles (clothing retail).
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The bidder models expected late conversions from your historical delay distribution. It does not treat missing recent conversions as failures. The bid strategy report shows conversions that are predicted but not yet reported, and flags the date range where more are expected.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** The bidder is designed to be patient. Humans who react to the last 3–7 days of CPA often undo what the bidder would have done right. Before touching targets, check the delay profile.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The delay model breaks when the delay pattern changes. Examples: a new offline-import cadence, a CRM upload that stalls, a new conversion action with a different lag, or a change in conversion window. The bidder then under- or over-predicts. Data exclusions exist for clear outages (PLT-BID-022).
- **Signals in data:** Hover on Conversions or Cost/conv. in the bid strategy report to see average delay and predicted unreported conversions. A shaded "conversions may still arrive" range on the chart.
- **Sources:** [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Find out how long it takes for your customers to convert](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6239119?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-014 · Why recent CPA looks bad: reporting by click date vs conversion time
- **Claim:** The standard Conversions column credits a conversion to the date of the **click** (interaction). So the last N days, where N is the conversion delay, always show full cost but incomplete conversions: CPA is inflated and ROAS deflated until late conversions arrive, up to 90 days after the click depending on the conversion window. The "Conversions (by conv. time)" columns credit conversions to the day they *happened*. Those days are complete, but they mix conversions from clicks bought at different times and prices.
- **Evidence type:** documented (delay and click-date attribution); inference (by-conv-time trade-off; its help page was not opened)
- **Confidence:** high (delay effect); medium (column definitions)
- **How it works:** Spend is reported in real time; conversions are reported later. Google: "your recent performance might not look as strong because of conversion delay". To check delay, segment by Conversions > Days to conversion, with a date range ending at least 30 days ago. That segment shows up to 19 rows.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Google's worked example: with a 2-day delay, judge historical CPA or ROAS over 28 days and leave out the most recent 2 days. Practitioners compare equal "matured" windows and use by-conv-time columns for pacing (did today produce sales?), but use click-date columns for judging efficiency.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Lead-gen with offline imports can have delays of weeks. A "bad last week" may simply be uploads not yet made. Comparing a matured month with an immature month always favours the older month.
- **Signals in data:** The last few days' CPA falls in later views (restatement). A large gap between Conversions and Conversions (by conv. time) for recent dates.
- **Sources:** [Tips on measuring Smart Bidding performance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268633) (checked 2026-10-13); [Find out how long it takes for your customers to convert](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6239119?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-015 · Evaluation windows and the "average target vs actual" comparison
- **Claim:** Google's guidance on evaluation windows: use a time frame with at least 2 full conversion cycles; for a clearer view, at least 30 conversions over a month or more (50 for Target ROAS), or "at least 50 conversions" in the measuring-tips page. Allow 1–2 conversion cycles after major bid or budget changes. Judge efficiency, not "trailing, dependent metrics like cost-per-click and impressions".
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Short windows mix sampling noise with conversion delay. The bid strategy report compares actual CPA or ROAS with the *average* target, and says to widen the range when the gap is not statistically significant.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Measure against Avg. target CPA or ROAS. Do not treat a CPC rise as a failure under Smart Bidding: the bidder may pay more per click for traffic that converts better. For a real causal read, use campaign experiments. Google offers "Save as experiment"; Optmyzr recommends at least 3 conversion cycles or 4 weeks, and often 4–8 weeks.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The 30 and 50 figures are evaluation guides, not eligibility thresholds. Very low-volume campaigns may never reach them, and should then be judged at portfolio or account level.
- **Signals in data:** Scorecard in the bid strategy report (Avg. target vs Actual), and the conversion count in the window.
- **Sources:** [About Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) (checked 2026-10-13); [Tips on measuring Smart Bidding performance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268633) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategy report](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7074566) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-016 · Setting the first target
- **Claim:** Start with targets "that align with your historical CPA or ROAS". For Target CPA, use the last 30 days, adjusted for delay. For Target ROAS, the target should be "at or below" historical ROAS, based on 4 weeks or 3 conversion cycles of value data (whichever is longer). "Overly-aggressive targets can affect your volume". When there are fewer than 7 conversions, Google calculates no recommended target.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The bidder's starting point is calibrated to current conversion rates. A target far tighter than history forces it to bid only on rare high-probability auctions, so volume collapses and there is less data to learn from.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Practitioners set the first target at or slightly looser than the matured historical average. They tighten later only if volume holds. Base the target on business economics (break-even CPA, margin-based ROAS), checked against what history shows is reachable. Optmyzr gives an example of an arbitrary 400% ROAS target set "because the last agency hit 300%", with no link to profit.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** History from Manual CPC with different query coverage may not carry over. A move to broad match or AI Max changes the reachable CPA.
- **Signals in data:** A new target strategy with sharply lower impressions in the first week usually means the target is too aggressive.
- **Sources:** [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Tips on measuring Smart Bidding performance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268633) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-017 · Changing targets: immediate response vs the "20% every two weeks" rule
- **Claim:** "Smart Bidding reacts immediately to any target change by adjusting bids." Reaching the new target can take 1–2 conversion cycles, and "in some rare cases, you may see some volatility for a conversion cycle or two". To increase spend, raise the CPA target "by the same proportion that you want to increase bids". The Target ROAS page still says to *gradually* lower the target to get more volume. The common practitioner rule, "change targets by no more than 15–20% at a time, every 1–2 weeks", is a heuristic. In 2026 Google said that aligning a target with recent performance need not be phased in.
- **Evidence type:** documented (immediate reaction, proportional change); practitioner (step-size rule)
- **Confidence:** high (documented); medium (heuristic)
- **How it works:** A target change rescales every auction's bid at once. Large jumps send the bidder into auctions it has little data on (new query/position mix), so realised efficiency can overshoot for a cycle. Optmyzr reports Google is phasing out the old "no more than 20% every two weeks" guidance. PPC Land reports Google advising against several ROAS target changes within one conversion cycle.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Experts change one variable at a time and wait one conversion cycle before the next move. They use bigger steps when the new target matches observed performance (e.g. after the August 2026 change), and smaller steps when moving into untested territory (much looser or much tighter targets).
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** In low-volume accounts, small steps cannot be told apart from noise, so step size matters less than waiting long enough. Repeated small changes within a cycle mean you never see the result of any one of them.
- **Signals in data:** Change history versus volatility in daily CPA or ROAS. Avg. target column with step changes.
- **Sources:** [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target ROAS bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC Land: 6 weeks before CPA targets double](https://ppc.land/google-ads-gives-advertisers-6-weeks-before-cpa-targets-double/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** No controlled study was found that compares large single changes with phased changes.

### PLT-BID-018 · Minimum conversion volumes: documented thresholds vs heuristics
- **Claim:** Google documents several different numbers for different purposes. **Eligibility/recommended history** for Target ROAS: 15 conversions in 30 days for Search and Shopping, and different figures for other campaign types (PLT-BID-006). **Evaluation:** 30 conversions over a month or more, or 50 for Target ROAS. **Calibration:** around 50 conversion events (en-GB, 2026). Target CPA and Maximize conversions can start with zero history. Practitioners usually want 30–50 conversions a month per strategy (Optmyzr). That is a heuristic, not a platform rule.
- **Evidence type:** documented (each figure in its own context); practitioner (30–50/month)
- **Confidence:** high (documented); medium (heuristic)
- **How it works:** Fewer conversions mean wider uncertainty in predicted conversion rates. The bidder leans more on query-level and account-wide priors, so it is less tailored to the campaign.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Pool volume through portfolios, consolidated campaigns or account-level data before dropping Smart Bidding. Mention the threshold that fits the decision: switching, evaluating, or judging calibration.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Account-wide learning means a new campaign in a high-volume account may do well with no history of its own. High-value B2B accounts with 5 conversions a month may still do better on Maximize conversions than on Manual CPC; there is no universal cut-off.
- **Signals in data:** Conversions per strategy per 30 days. Wide day-to-day swings in CPA at low volume.
- **Sources:** [About Target ROAS bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-019 · Target-based strategies with too few conversions
- **Claim:** With little data, Smart Bidding starts from query-level conversion-rate models and data from all of your campaigns, then refines them with Bayesian learning. A target strategy with too few conversions therefore bids mostly on priors. Whether the target is met in a given month is largely noise.
- **Evidence type:** documented (mechanism); inference (consequences)
- **Confidence:** medium
- **How it works:** Bayesian shrinkage pulls sparse campaign-level estimates toward broader priors (query-level, account-level). The bidder moves away from the prior only as conversions accumulate. Very tight targets on sparse data tend to under-deliver, because the bidder cannot confidently find auctions that clear the bar.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Options practitioners use: (1) Maximize conversions without a target, under budget control, until volume builds. (2) A portfolio strategy that pools campaigns with similar economics. (3) Adding a higher-volume but well-correlated primary action. This trades off goal quality, and the proxy must predict revenue (inference). (4) Consolidating campaigns. Google-documented minimum histories still apply to Target ROAS.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Mixing micro-conversions into the primary set can make the bidder optimise toward cheap, low-quality actions. Pooling campaigns with very different economics into one target hides losses.
- **Signals in data:** A target strategy with fewer than about 15 conversions in 30 days, little spend, and status Limited or Learning for long periods.
- **Sources:** [How our bidding algorithms learn](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10970825?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7065882) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-020 · Portfolio strategies, shared budgets and bid limits
- **Claim:** Portfolio strategies apply one goal to several campaigns, ad groups and keywords. Supported: Maximize conversions, Maximize conversion value, Target CPA, Target ROAS, Maximize clicks and Target IS. Not available for Performance Max (nor Target ROAS portfolios for Hotel/Travel). Cross-account portfolios exist at manager level. Max/min CPC **bid limits** for Target CPA and Target ROAS exist only in **portfolio** strategies, apply only to Search Network auctions, and are not recommended by Google. Smart pricing can push a CPC below the min limit. If campaigns sharing a budget use different Maximize strategies, the status shows **Misconfigured**.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** A portfolio optimises toward the target across all its members, so individual campaigns may sit above or below target while the portfolio averages out (inference from "average target"). Bid limits clip the bidder's auction-level bids. A max limit removes high-value auctions, and a min limit forces overpaying on low-value ones.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use portfolios to pool sparse campaigns with the same economics, or to manage several campaigns under one CPA. Use bid limits only as guardrails against CPC spikes, for example during data outages or on low-trust traffic. Remove them when the status shows "Limited (bid limits)" and a large share of spend is capped. Turning on Smart Bidding Exploration at portfolio level applies it to every campaign in the portfolio.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Portfolios hide campaign-level losses when member economics differ. After August 2026, target adjustments for budget-limited campaigns must be made at portfolio level. The account-wide bid strategy report does not yet support portfolios.
- **Signals in data:** Campaigns in one portfolio whose CPAs are well apart. "Limited (bid limits)" with, e.g., 95% of spend capped by the max bid.
- **Sources:** [Portfolio bid strategy: Definition](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6263072?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target ROAS bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategy statuses](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6263057) (checked 2026-10-13); [Bid strategies to help you meet your goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10969287?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-021 · Bid strategy status messages and what to do
- **Claim:** The documented statuses are: **Inactive**; **Active**; **Learning** (new strategy, setting change, composition change, Shopping ad-group target change); **Limited** (inventory, bid limits, budget constrained, or bidding strategy, which on campaigns means "consider a fully automated strategy"); **Misconfigured** (Maximize strategies on a shared budget with different strategies; or a conversion setting such as missing website/call/app conversion actions or actions not included in "Conversions").

For a Maximize strategy, "limited by budget" is normal by design. For a target strategy, it means either add budget or accept target convergence (PLT-BID-007). Misconfigured (conversion setting) means bidding has no valid signal: fix tracking first.

### PLT-BID-022 · Data exclusions: only for broken conversion data
- **Claim:** Data exclusions tell Smart Bidding to ignore clicks, and the conversions from them, during a conversion-data problem: tracking or tag failures, site outages, failed offline uploads, or planned maintenance. Excluded data still appears in reports. The window must cover the conversion delay. Google's example: a problem from 15–18 Oct with a 5-day delay needs an exclusion from 10–18 Oct, and should cover at least 90% of affected clicks. Do not use them often, do not remove them once applied, and do not backfill data for bidding.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** Excluded clicks are removed from the training data, so the bidder does not learn a falsely low conversion rate. Bids and spend may dip while an exclusion is active. Google suggests temporarily lowering the target ROAS (then restoring it) and keeping budgets near historical spend for a few cycles. Works for Search, Display, Shopping and PMax; not Hotel.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use them right after you discover a tracking break. Not for "bad weeks", promotions or seasonality: Google explicitly advises against using them as a reaction to the August 2026 bidding change. Past-date exclusions usually stabilise within days. More than a week of affected clicks can cause 1–2 cycles of fluctuation.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Over-use starves the model of real data. An exclusion window that misses the delay period leaves corrupted data in place.
- **Signals in data:** A sudden drop to zero (or a spike) in one conversion action while traffic is normal. Gaps in offline import logs.
- **Sources:** [About data exclusions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10370710?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [FAQ about changes to Target-based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17125145) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-023 · Seasonality adjustments: documented use
- **Claim:** Seasonality adjustments tell Smart Bidding to expect a conversion-rate change over a set period. Google says they are "ideal for short events of 1–7 days" and may work less well over more than 14 days. Use them only for major expected changes, because "Smart Bidding already manages seasonal events". Google's example: a 3-day sale where you expect conversion rates up to 50% higher gets a +50% adjustment. No negative adjustment is needed afterwards.
- **Evidence type:** documented
- **Confidence:** high
- **How it works:** The adjustment overrides the predicted conversion rate for the window, scaling bids by the stated factor. Afterwards the bidder returns to normal, and per Tinuiti those days are treated as abnormal in its look-back. Supported for Target CPA and Target ROAS on Search, Shopping and Display; all strategies on PMax; App in beta; not Travel. Can be applied from a manager account across clients.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Use it for events the bidder cannot see coming: a one-off flash sale, an email-only promotion, a surprise clearance, a sudden price cut. A negative adjustment can cover a known short conversion-rate slump. Tinuiti set −20–35% for the days after Cyber Monday and reported ROAS +46% year on year; the result is anecdotal and uncontrolled.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Over-estimating the lift leads to overbidding. Adjustments applied at manager level persist in accounts that leave the manager.
- **Signals in data:** CPC jump at the start of the window with conversion-rate lift smaller than the adjustment.
- **Sources:** [About seasonality adjustments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10369906) (checked 2026-10-13); [Tinuiti: Google Seasonality Adjustments](https://tinuiti.com/blog/paid-search/google-seasonality-adjustments/) (checked 2026-10-13)

### PLT-BID-024 · Seasonality adjustments on BFCM: evidence they hurt efficiency
- **Claim:** An Optmyzr study of about 4,300–5,900 accounts per year (BFCM 2022–2024, observational) found that accounts **without** adjustments still saw the BFCM conversion-rate lift (+17.5%, +11.9%, +7.5%) and kept ROAS roughly flat (−2% to +5.7%). Accounts **with** adjustments saw about twice the CPC inflation (+32–37% vs +16–17%) and double-digit ROAS drops (−10% to −17%), but higher revenue growth (+40–53% vs +25–34%).
- **Evidence type:** study (vendor, observational)
- **Confidence:** medium
- **How it works:** Smart Bidding already sees the BFCM lift through real-time signals and prior years. An adjustment on top double-counts it, or overstates it: predicting +50% when the actual is +40% means overbidding by about 7% (1.5/1.4).
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Default: no adjustment for predictable, market-wide events (BFCM, Christmas, Valentine's Day for florists). Use one only when you deliberately want volume at lower efficiency, such as clearing inventory. **(changed 2026-06)** Google added "promotion mode" (beta, Search and PMax). It schedules a temporary ROAS tolerance plus extra budget for peaks. It is separate from seasonality adjustments, and how the two interact is not documented.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The study does not match cohorts or report CPA. Advertisers who used adjustments may differ systematically, for example by being more promotion-heavy. The study's author (Optmyzr's CEO) sells monitoring tools. Revenue growth was higher with adjustments, so the "harm" applies only under an efficiency objective.
- **Signals in data:** CPC inflation over the BFCM window that is much larger than the CVR lift. ROAS below the prior-year BFCM ROAS.
- **Sources:** [Optmyzr: Do Seasonality Adjustments Actually Help During BFCM?](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/bfcm-seasonality-adjustments-study/) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: Why seasonality adjustments keep failing advertisers on Black Friday](https://searchengineland.com/study-why-seasonality-adjustments-keep-failing-advertisers-on-black-friday-465112) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC Land: Promotion mode and a major bidding overhaul](https://ppc.land/google-ads-gets-promotion-mode-and-a-major-bidding-overhaul-this-august/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** How promotion mode's ROAS tolerance interacts with seasonality adjustments and Exploration.

### PLT-BID-026 · Conversion value rules: real-time bid control that also changes reported ROAS
- **Claim:** Conversion value rules adjust conversion values (e.g. "increase value for users in New York by 20%", or ×2 for a valuable audience). Conditions can be audience, location or device, and each rule can have a primary and a secondary condition. The adjusted values are used **in real time** by Target ROAS and Maximize conversion value, *and* are reported in the Conversion value column. ### PLT-BID-028 · Bid, budget and target simulators: what they model
- **Claim:** Simulators estimate what the **previous 7 days** would have looked like with different bids, budgets or targets. They use auction data including Quality Score, keyword traffic and competition at the time, plus any active bid adjustments. The Search, PMax and App simulators include conversions expected to arrive later; the other simulators count only conversions recorded in the window. - **Confidence:** high (what's documented); medium (what is not modelled)

### PLT-BID-029 · Smart Bidding Exploration: how it works
- **Claim:** **(changed 2025-05; expanded 2026-06)** Smart Bidding Exploration was announced 21 May 2025 at Google Marketing Live as "our biggest update to bidding in over a decade". It is an opt-in setting for **Target ROAS Search** campaigns and portfolios. A **ROAS tolerance between 5% and 30%** lowers the effective target: 200% with 10% tolerance gives an effective 180%. With the extra room, the bidder bids on queries from more distinct "search categories" (groups of queries with the same intent) within existing broad match, DSA or AI Max coverage. It does not expand match reach. In June 2026 it became generally available for PMax without a product feed and entered beta for feed-based PMax and standard Shopping.
- **Evidence type:** documented; google-statement (results)
- **Confidence:** high (mechanism); low (results)
- **How it works:** A lower effective ROAS target lets the bidder buy auctions whose predicted value is uncertain, which builds data on new query categories. New metrics, "unique Search categories with impressions/clicks/conversions", appear in the bid strategy report and as columns, and can be de-duplicated. Google's result: +18% unique converting query categories and +19% conversions on average. That is Google internal data, 11 Mar–11 Apr 2025, with no control group described.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Google's guidance: run on established campaigns, not new ones. Avoid CPC bid limits. Do not raise the target ROAS to offset the tolerance. Run for at least 6 weeks (or an experiment of 6+ weeks, plus 1–2 weeks to ramp). Judge by traffic diversity and conversion volume, with actual ROAS close to the *lowered* average target. It does nothing if the campaign is limited by budget.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** The ROAS dip is the price of exploring. Whether new categories pay off over time is unproven. Enabling it at portfolio level enables it for every member campaign. PPC Land wrongly says it has worked on Search "since 2024".
- **Signals in data:** Avg. target ROAS below the set target. Rising unique-category counts. An alert when the campaign is budget-limited.
- **Sources:** [Google blog: Smart Bidding Exploration](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/smart-bidding-exploration-ai/) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding Exploration (overview)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15489627?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding Exploration (best practices)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16294686?hl=en-GB) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding Exploration (setup)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16294612?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Smart Bidding Exploration (measurement)](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16294226) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: Google Smart Bidding Exploration](https://searchengineland.com/google-smart-bidding-exploration-455756) (checked 2026-10-13); [PPC Land: Promotion mode and Exploration expansion](https://ppc.land/google-ads-gets-promotion-mode-and-a-major-bidding-overhaul-this-august/) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** Whether Target CPA will get an equivalent. Whether the explored categories keep converting after Exploration is turned off.

### PLT-BID-030 · Scaling: marginal vs average CPA/ROAS and diminishing returns
- **Claim:** Reported CPA and ROAS are **averages**. Each extra unit of spend, from a higher budget on a Maximize strategy or a looser target, buys conversions at a **marginal** cost that is usually much higher than the average. Google implies the concave curve itself: Target CPA buys individual conversions above and below target, and the simulators show cost/volume curves. Scaling decisions should use marginal CPA/ROAS against marginal economics, not the blended figure.
- **Evidence type:** inference (from documented averaging and simulator mechanics); practitioner (common in expert writing, but no independent source was opened this session)
- **Confidence:** medium
- **How it works:** The bidder ranks auctions by expected value per cost. More spend reaches further down that ranking: lower-probability queries, higher positions at higher CPCs, weaker audiences. Example (illustrative): moving from 100 conversions at $50 CPA ($5,000) to 120 at $55 ($6,600) means the 20 extra conversions cost $1,600/20 = $80 each. If break-even is $70, the "fine-looking" $55 average hides unprofitable marginal spend.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Scale in steps. Measure Δcost/Δconversions after delay has matured, or from simulator points. Stop when marginal CPA reaches break-even, or marginal ROAS reaches 1/margin. Headroom checks: for target strategies, look at Search lost IS (rank) and (budget) and the status. For Maximize strategies, "lost IS (budget)" is not meaningful (Google), so use the budget simulator or Performance Planner. Since August 2026, raising the budget on a budget-limited target campaign scales close to the target (Google's "$7 custom target" example), which makes budget-led scaling more predictable.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Seasonal demand growth can lower marginal CPA temporarily. Structural changes such as new keywords, broad match or AI Max add new inventory, so the curve shifts instead of the account moving along it. Comparing periods with different conversion maturity creates false marginal readings.
- **Signals in data:** CPC and CPA rising faster than conversions after a budget or target change. Rising absolute-top IS with flat conversions. Simulator curves flattening.
- **Sources:** [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Maximize conversions](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7381968) (checked 2026-10-13); [Estimate your results with simulators](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2470105?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13); [Changes to target based bid strategies](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17061251?hl=en) (checked 2026-10-13)
- **Open questions:** No published dataset quantifying typical marginal-to-average CPA ratios for budget or target increases was opened in this session.

### PLT-BID-031 · Conversion goal and value changes recalibrate the bidder
- **Claim:** Changing which actions count as primary conversions, or how values are set, changes the bidder's objective. Google's learning-period guidance on conversion-goal changes now explicitly covers Search, Shopping and PMax. **(changed 2026-09)** For Maximize conversion value, Google cut the recommended wait before adopting newly reported values from "4 weeks or 3 conversion cycles" (Search) to "1–2 conversion cycles" (Search and Shopping).
- **Evidence type:** documented (via industry coverage of the help-page edits); google-statement
- **Confidence:** medium (the Maximize conversion value page was not opened directly)
- **How it works:** Smart Bidding learns across every action in the Conversions column. A new primary action with a different rate, lag or value changes the predictions everywhere it applies.
- **When it matters / how experts use it:** Treat goal changes as major changes: one at a time, with a full evaluation window afterwards. Keep secondary (observation) actions out of "Conversions" so they cannot influence bidding.
- **Exceptions and failure modes:** Value stacking across several primary actions on one purchase (Optmyzr example: $10 + $20 + $50 = $80 for one sale).
- **Signals in data:** CPA or ROAS step changes on the date of a goal change. A conversions-per-click jump without a revenue change.
- **Sources:** [SER: Google Ads reduces conversion cycle for Maximize Conversion Value](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ads-conversion-cycle-max-conv-41919.html) (checked 2026-10-13); [SEL: reframes learning around 50 conversions](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-reframes-smart-bidding-learning-around-50-conversions-490382) (checked 2026-10-13); [About Target CPA bidding](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268632) (checked 2026-10-13); [Optmyzr: Value-Based Bidding guide](https://www.optmyzr.com/blog/value-based-bidding-guide/) (checked 2026-10-13)

## Common mistakes and misleading metrics
- **Judging the last 3–7 days of CPA or ROAS** without allowing for conversion delay. Recent click-date data is always incomplete (PLT-BID-013, PLT-BID-014).
- **Comparing actual CPA with the typed target** instead of Avg. target CPA or ROAS, which includes device adjustments and mid-period changes (PLT-BID-005, PLT-BID-025).
- **Treating the old over-performance of budget-capped target campaigns as normal.** Since August 2026 they drift to the stated target; stale targets now cost money (PLT-BID-007).
- **Raising the budget on a target-constrained or inventory-limited campaign** and expecting volume. Check the status reason first (PLT-BID-021).
- **Reading "Search lost IS (budget)" for Maximize strategies.** Google says these are budget-limited by design and the column is not recommended for them (PLT-BID-004).
- **Using average CPA or ROAS for scaling decisions.** The marginal cost of extra volume is higher (PLT-BID-030).
- **Seeing CPC rises under Smart Bidding as failure.** CPC and impressions are "trailing, dependent metrics" (PLT-BID-015).
- **ROAS inflated by construction** through conversion value rules, new-customer bonus value, or stacked primary actions with values (PLT-BID-026, PLT-BID-027, PLT-BID-031).
- **Using data exclusions for bad weeks or promotions,** or removing them later (PLT-BID-022).
- **Adding seasonality adjustments for BFCM,** which the bidder already anticipates (PLT-BID-024).
- **Treating "any edit resets learning", "7-day learning" or "30 conversions/month" as platform rules.** Target changes do not trigger Learning. Duration is set by conversion cycles and volume (PLT-BID-011, PLT-BID-012, PLT-BID-018).
- **Advising ECPC as a stepping stone.** It no longer exists for Search or Display (PLT-BID-002).
- **Using Maximize clicks for conversion goals.** It values every click equally (PLT-BID-009).
